The app

LocalRIM in practice: searching, reading, filing and reviewing records

The LocalRIM application brings document processing, classification, search and review into one interface. Its Archive contains filed Philip Morris records. A user can search and examine records, ask questions answered with citations, and process new documents through the classification and review workflow.

A local, single-user application, developed with public archive documents and not deployed for a client.

What the video shows

The walkthrough begins in the Archive, where a question about safety data sheets produces an answer with citations to supporting records. The Reader opens a cited document alongside its filing details and the text used in the answer. Browse then filters the Archive by department, series and record type.

Three new pages are uploaded. Two meet the confidence cutoff and wait in Intake for approval; the third is routed to the Review queue, where a reviewer examines the suggested filing and supporting records before deciding.

All documents shown come from the public UCSF archive. No private client records are used.

Three views for using the archive, three for adding to it

Using the archive

Archive and Ask

Search the filed records or ask questions using retrieval-augmented generation. Questions and documents are represented with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B to retrieve relevant records; Qwen3 8B uses those records to write a concise answer with citations, and declines questions the Archive cannot support.

Reader

Open a record as the scanned page, its extracted text, or both side by side, with its department, series, record type and file ID. A filing can be confirmed or changed; each change is kept in the record's history, and the original filing is never overwritten.

Watch 0:57

Browse

Filter and sort records by department, series, record type and date. Filings produced by the pipeline stay marked as suggested until a person confirms them.

Watch 1:28

Adding to it

Add files

Upload new pages for reading and classification. PaddleOCR reads each page, and the pipeline suggests a department, series and record type. Qwen3 8B also suggests a title and document date, and the page's file number is read for the duplicate check.

Watch 1:56

Intake

Pages with readable text, at least 20 tokens, confidence at or above the cutoff and a file number not already known wait in Intake. A person approves them as a batch before they are added to the Archive.

Watch 2:27

Review queue

Pages go to the Review queue when their confidence is below , they have fewer than 20 tokens or no readable text, or their file number matches one already in the Archive, the queue or an earlier upload. The reviewer sees the page, the suggested filing and similar filed records before deciding where it belongs.

Median response time by round

I evaluated the archive assistant in two rounds of answerable questions, together with questions about material outside the Archive. The first round used questions written from known records; the second used questions written before searching for supporting documents.

The assistant cited a correct or relevant record in of the answerable questions and declined all questions about material outside the Archive. Citing a relevant record did not guarantee a fully supported answer: I judged answers fully supported, partly supported and unsupported, and question was declined. The answers without a relevant citation were an unsupported inference, a wrong record cited, and a question the search could not match.

This was a limited check on a fixed set of questions, not a full evaluation of answer reliability (see Future work).

Local processing keeps records on the same computer

LocalRIM processes documents and runs its models locally. PaddleOCR extracts text, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B represents pages and questions for search and classification, and Qwen3 8B, served through Ollama, writes answers and review suggestions. The records and their extracted text stay on the local computer and are not sent to an external model service.

The application is designed for a single user. Hosting it as it is would mean sending document data to a server, so private hosted access remains future work.
