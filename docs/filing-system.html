How to read the figures

Figures use a consistent set of marks to distinguish confirmed labels, uncertain cases, and supporting context.

  • Solid: filed automatically, or labeled with certainty.
  • Hatched topaz: routed for review, or manually labeled as uncertain.
  • Crimson outline: incorrect departmental classification.
  • Solid taupe: contextual information without a classification outcome.
  • One square: One page. Counts and charts do not display document images, file names, or page IDs.

How the sample was selected

I began with a general taxonomy and tested it on a public benchmark containing documents from several companies. The approach proved unsuitable because each company organized its records differently, making it difficult to apply one set of categories consistently. I therefore focused the project on Philip Morris as the client, using its retention schedules and records-management documents to develop the final taxonomy. The documents were then drawn at random from the Philip Morris Records collection in the UCSF archive, restricted to publicly available scanned records and selected using a fixed seed.

How the labels were made

I built a Streamlit application to label the documents and refined it through seven stages as the work progressed. Did rebuilding the categories reduce labeling uncertainty?

Two pilot rounds examined labeling uncertainty under different classification schemes. The first used generic categories; the second used categories derived from Philip Morris's departmental structure. Each label was also marked as certain or uncertain, allowing the proportion of uncertain decisions to be compared between rounds.

How the reviewed documents were labeled or excluded

Each reviewed document was either labeled or excluded for a recorded reason: blank or unreadable content, a folder cover without contents, or a record outside the project's scope. The interactive figure shows these outcomes for the full set and individual labeling batches.

How the labels were distributed across departments

The figures show the original departmental labels assigned during manual review. For model evaluation, Administration was merged into Legal (D-27); the original counts displayed here remain unchanged. Human Resources is out of scope (D-39): only of the reviewed documents belonged to it, too few to model, and both were excluded.

Which departments overlap in the labeled documents?

When a document could reasonably be assigned to a second department, that alternative was recorded alongside its primary label. These overlaps identify potentially ambiguous departmental boundaries. Figure 3.6 examines whether the classifier made errors across the same boundaries.
