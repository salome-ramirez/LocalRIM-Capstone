PHILIP MORRIS U.S.A.
INTER -OFFICE CORRESPONDENCE
100 Park Avenue, New York, N.Y, 10017
J. J. Gillis Date: August 21, 1980
From: G. A. Choyke
Subject: Professional Retailer's Organization, Inc.
John Heard, AMCs in Section 33, has been contacted by Mr.
Frank Beyerly, founder and president of Professional
Retailer's Organization, Inc., for the purpose of entering
into merchandising agreements with Philip Morris U.S.A. on
behalf of his clients, which are independent drug stores.
P.R.O., Inc., performs certain buying and in-store merchan-
dising functions for retailers under a contractual agree-
ment, a copy of which is attached. Currently, P.R.O., Inc.,
has 50 drug stores under contract. Paragraph three of the
agreement permits P.R.O., Inc., to "negotiate for and
collect all allowances and fees from any manufacturer,
distributor, wholesaler or other supplier earned by the
retail merchant - to include but not limited to retail
display allowances." Such allowances are then divided
equally with the retailer.
Mr. Beyerly has advised me that Brown & Williamson has
signed a counter display agreement with him.
As stated earlier, P.R.O., Inc.,'would like to sign mer-
chandising agreements (Plan B and/or Plan A) and receive
payment for stores complying, in a manner similar to a
chain headquarters contracting for franchise operations.
After preliminary discussion with Beyerly, John Heard
called Joanne Cunningham in the New York Office, who in
turn contacted me, to ascertain whether we could sign a
contract with P.R.O., Inc. I advised John that we would
like to see a copy of any agreement that P.R.O., Inc.,
has with its clients. John called Beyerly, who in turn
called me, and at my request sent the attached contract.
I have also reguested the section to send me a list of
the stores involved with indication of any current mer-
chandising plans in effect. I understand that few, if
any, have a Plan B now.
I reviewed the document with Carl Adkins, and he is of
the opinion that Philip Morris may or may not enter into
merchandising agreements with P.R.O., Inc., dependent
upon management's judgement as to whether it would be a
sound business practice.
