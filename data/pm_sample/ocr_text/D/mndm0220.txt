Research Abstract

Project Title: AP-1 signaling in cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and apoptosis

Investigator(s): Prof. Dr. Dr. H. M. Piper (PI), Dr. G. Taimor (CoPI)

Institution: Physiological Institute, Justus-Liebig-University, Giessen

Abstract: In the space below, please provide a descriptive summary of your proposed research project.

The pathogenesis of heart failure is still only poorly understood. The transition from compensated hypertrophy to failure is accompanied by a progressive apoptotic loss of cardiomyocytes. Interestingly, stimuli of hypertrophy as well as apoptosis share the AP-1 signaling complex as a central regulatory element. AP-1 is a composite transcription factor, whose subunit composition and interaction with other transcription factors may vary. It can be hypothesized that under conditions favoring cardiac growth or apoptosis the composition of the AP-1 complex differs and, due to this difference, different AP-1 controlled pathways become activated. Variations in AP-1 transcription complexes may trigger the transition from hypertrophy to apoptosis in the myocardial cells.

It is the central aim of this project to identify on the cellular level the different compositions of AP-1 complexes under hypertrophy and apoptosis inducing conditions. Isolated cardiomyocytes from adult rat are used as model. The α-adrenoceptor agonist phenylephrine and the NO-donor SNAP will be used as exemplatory stimuli for induction of AP-1 mediated hypertrophy and apoptosis, respectively. The expression of AP-1 components, of interacting transcription factors, of target genes controlled by these specific AP-1 complexes and AP-1 driven “downstream” genes specific for one or the other kind of stimulus will be identified with suitable molecular techniques. The causal roles of transcription factors and other genes will be tested with use of decoy or antisense oligonucleotides. It will also be tested if adenoviral overexpression of identified pro-apoptotic AP-1 components or dependent genes can transform hypertrophic stimuli into apoptotic processes or if pro-hypertrophic genes protect the cells against apoptotic stimuli. Finally, the relevance of the results obtained in the cellular model will be evaluated in an established in vivo model of cardiac hypertrophy and failure, i.e. the SHR-sp rat. The expression of the previously identified transcription factors and other genes, related to hypertrophy or apoptosis, will be analyzed in these rats at the stages of compensated hypertrophy, transition to heart failure and at manifest failure. A provocation test performed on hearts isolated from these animals will functionally demonstrate their relative propensity for apoptosis.

In summary, the project is designed to analyze on the molecular level the role of AP-1 signaling as a central switch for induction of hypertrophy or apoptosis and heart failure.
