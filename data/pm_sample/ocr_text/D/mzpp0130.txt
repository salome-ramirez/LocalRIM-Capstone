PROPOSAL II

Relevance of the N-nitroso Compounds from the Reaction of Primary Amines with Nitrite and Thiocyanate to the Smoker

Tannenbaum et al. (1977) consider the reaction products of primary amines in food on one hand and nitrite and thiocyanate, both present in the saliva, on the other, to be "of potential significance in the context of human cancer". The authors present significant evidence that N-nitroso compounds - N-nitroso dialkylamines and N-nitroso cyano amines - are among those reaction products.

Primary amines are abundant in tobacco smoke (Neurath et al. 1966, Pailer et al. 1967, Schmeltz and Hoffmann, 1977).

Nitrite is always present in human saliva, the quantity directly correlated to the dietary intake of nitrate (Stephany and Schuller, 1975, Spiegelhalder et al. 1976, Tannenbaum et al. 1976).

Thiocyanate has been found to be three to four times enhanced in the saliva of smokers compared to its concentration in the saliva of non-smokers (Densen et al. 1967, Boyland and Walker, 1974, Walters et al. 1977) and is known to catalyse the nitrosation reaction of secondary amines with nitrite, increasing the rate of reaction about 550 times at $p_H$ 1.5 (Boyland and Walker, 1974).

Therefore, the formation of N-nitroso compounds from primary amines in the smokers gastro-intestinal tract is to be considered a potential second path of possible relevance of this class of carcinogens to the smoker.
