Memo: To Mr. W. F. Greenwald.

Re: Dr. Emil Bogen Whose work is cited in a brochure published by the Axton-Fisher Tobacco Company, Louisville, Kentucky. Entitled "Theory and Facts on Cigarette Smoking" by Otis Allen Kenyon.

REVIEW OF CHEMICAL ABSTRACTS

E. Bogen: C. A. 17:2896 - Clinical tests for liver functions. (Abstract not available)

E. Bogen: C. A. 24:468 - The Composition of cigarettes and cigarette smoke. J. Am. Med. Assoc. 93,1110-4 (1929).

The moisture content varied with changes in humidity and ranged from 8-50% by weight; the average of 20 brands as taken from the package was 12%. The inorganic ash is related to the source and, in general, bears an inverse relationship to the nicotine content. Average ash content: domestic brands, 11.9%; blends, 13.9%; oriental brands, 15.6%; denicotinized, 15.8%; West Indian, 18.3%; tobacco-free, 8.6%; Average nicotine content: domestic brands, 2.50%; blends, 1.9%; oriental, 1.44%; denicotinized, 1.10%; West Indian, 0.86%; tobacco-free, 0.00%. Composition of smoke varies according to the amount of suction: Analysis of "sidestream" smoke collected from the burning end without suction: duration of combustion, 12 min.; CO₂, 320 cc.; CO₂, 8 cc.; nicotine, 13 mg.; NH₃, 2.2 mg.; aldehyde, 1.4 mg.; furfural, 0.4 mg.; alkali, 0.1 N, 4.2 Co. Analysis of "mainstream" smoke drawn through the cigarette with intermittant suction: duration of combustion, 10 min.; CO₂, 21 cc.; DO, 3 cc.; nicotine, 0.2 mg.; NH₃, 0.0 mg.; aldehyde, 0.4 mg.; furfural, 0.0 mg.; acid, 0.1 N, 0.5 Co. The nicotine content of stumps smoked for 10 min. with intermittant suction was 6.8 mg.

E. Bogen: C. A. 26, 1564: Hydraulic apparatus for tilting converters for steel manufacture. (Patent)

Emil Bogen & Russel N. Loomis; C. A. 27, 534: Tobacco tar: an experimental investigation of its alleged carcinogenic action. Am. J. Cancer 16, 1515-21 (1932).

No cancers or other marked changes were produced in the skin of mice and rabbits when painted with tar from tobacco over periods that often led to cancer formation in control animals painted with gas-house tar.
