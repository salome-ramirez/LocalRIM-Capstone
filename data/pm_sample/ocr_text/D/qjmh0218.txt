February 17, 1997

Dear Senator Rockefeller,

I am the president of the Club Association of West Virginia. This can be a difficult job, but being a retail merchant is even more difficult. I have come to view retail merchants as the infantrymen of our economy. They are the ones on the front-line; the ones who work the longest hours and the ones whose fortunes are most closely tied to the fluctuations of our economy. Like foot-soldiers, they sometimes have to follow rules devised by leaders who are out of touch with the conditions at the front.

I would like to draw your attention to the latest set of rules and regulations about smoking handed down from above, in this case the Food and Drug Administration. These rules, although they are well intended, would make conditions on the front lines a lot tougher than they need be. In my humble opinion, they are very bad for morale.

Members of my association are not ideologues or political activists. These proposed regulations upset them because they will have a direct affect on their businesses.

For example, many clubs have cigarette machines. I understand that they would be allowed to keep their machines, but most restaurants, bowling centers, and other such places would not. This would make each route more costly for the vending companies to maintain, and thus drive down the profits my members make from the machines.

Furthermore, those members with cigarette machines would have to go through and remove every item from their clubs that have tobacco brand names or logos on them. Every counter mat, change tray, wall clock, door decal and ashtray would have to go. My members would have to bear the financial cost of replacing them. That would cause a financial burden to most clubs, especially those which have just opened. Many struggling clubs use these promotional items to cut back on start-up costs.

Also, the FDA will outlaw brand name sponsorship of cultural, musical, social, and athletic events. This would not only end important national events like the Virginia Slims tournament, but it will also hurt many small local local events. I can think of many concerts, holiday celebrations and Oktoberfests that tobacco companies sponsor in local communities.
