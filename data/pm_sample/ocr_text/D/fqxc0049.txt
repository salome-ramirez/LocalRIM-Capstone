Tobacco maker offers limits aimed at minors

NEW YORK — The nation's largest tobacco company offered Wednesday to back sweeping legislation intended to cut tobacco use by minors, but only if the government drops efforts to regulate cigarettes.

The bid met with a lukewarm reception in Washington, where a White House spokesman said it "falls a little bit short" of President Clinton's goals.

Philip Morris USA's proposal would ban vending machine and mail-order sales of tobacco products.

The maker of Marlboro also would seek a ban on all outdoor advertising of tobacco products within 1,000 feet of a school or a playground, and would limit advertising at sporting events.

All cigarette advertising would be prohibited on trains, buses, subways and taxis.

The plan would make it a federal crime to sell cigarettes to people under 18 and the proposed limits would make it impossible for anyone to buy cigarettes "without a face-to-face transaction where proof of age can be checked," Philip Morris spokesman Steven Parrish said.

But Philip Morris said it would only support the legislation if the Food and Drug Administration is precluded from having jurisdiction over virtually all tobacco products.

The exception would be a tobacco product making a health claim.

The FDA has proposed declaring that cigarettes are a drug-delivery device, and therefore subject to its jurisdiction. The cigarette maker argues the FDA's proposals violate federal law and the Constitution.

Parrish said Philip Morris was willing to support limits on advertising as long as FDA regulation is not involved.

Philip Morris was joined in its announcement by United States Tobacco Co., which makes smokeless tobacco products.
