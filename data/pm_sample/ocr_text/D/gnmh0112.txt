I. FLAVOR CHENISTRY

The enolate of cyclopentanone, generated via the reaction of cyclopentanone with potassium hydride, was treated with 2-chloroprazine to give 2-(2-pyrazyl)cyclopentanone. Reduction of the pyrazyl betone with sodium borohydride gave a mixture of cis- and trans-2-(2-pyrazyl)cyclopentanol. The pure epimeric alcohols were obtained by preparative tlc. A preliminary study of the decomposition of the alcohols at 170° indicates that their decomposition is extremely slow at this temperature.

Limited subjective testing of cigarettes injected with an alcohol solution of poly(monomethyl itaconate) has given indications that this polymer may well be a useful menthol-release agent. Mainstream delivery of menthol from the itaconate polymer appears to be approximately equivalent to the delivery from poly(isopropenyl menthyl carbonate). The advantage of the itaconate polymer is not only its alcohol solubility, but its lower cost as well. Studies are underway to design a synthesis of the monomer which is more amenable to scale up than the method presently being used.

Samples of both 3-0-isovaleroyl-0-glucose and 1-0-isovaleroyl-0-mannose have been prepared. A preliminary pyrolysis of the latter compound demonstrated that isovaleric acid is the major volatile pyrolysis product.

The pyridine catalyzed reaction of t-butyl acetoacetate and menthyl chloroformate has been repeated. The semi solid product appears to be the trono-enol carbonate according to its nmr spectrum; however, tlc indicates the presence of a second component. The synthesis of t-butyl acetate was carried out in preparation for the synthesis of t-butyl acetate-1-4C.
