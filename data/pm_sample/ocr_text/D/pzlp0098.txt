INTRODUCTORY REMARKS TO THE CONRAD
SENATORIAL TASK FORCE

Mr. Chairman, my name is Louis Camilleri. I am Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer of Philip Morris Companies Inc. I would like to thank you for inviting me here today to discuss the economic aspects of the proposed national tobacco resolution and, specifically, to address the resolution’s impact on:

- retail prices
- cigarette consumption
- industry profits and industry stock prices.

We have submitted a full paper on this topic and accordingly I shall restrict my introductory remarks to the key conclusions of the report.

These are essentially as follows:

We conservatively project that retail prices will rise in nominal terms by an absolute minimum of $1.20/pack by 2002 and $1.52/pack by 2007.

In real terms, that is to say expressed in 1997 dollars, the price increase will be a minimum of $1.06/pack in 2002 and a minimum $1.19/pack in 2007.

I should point out that these retail price projections exclude any increases in state excise taxes, which have risen every year, encapsulate a conservative assumption on trade margins and contain no lookback surcharges.

Wall Street analysts project real retail price increases of between $1.10/pack and $1.18/pack by 2002 and $1.50/pack by 2007.

Thus the $0.62/pack figure that is often quoted is incorrect as it fails to reflect all the components of the price structure that applies to cigarettes.

Cigarette demand is a function of price, price gaps, the availability of substitute products, demographics, inflation, consumer disposable income, social attitudes towards smoking and public smoking restrictions, to name a few. Many of these parameters will be significantly impacted by the proposed resolution.

Any projection is a perilous exercise, but in this specific instance, it is particularly difficult to predict with any degree of confidence the proposed resolution’s effect on cigarette consumption. This inherent uncertainty arises from the unprecedented magnitude and scope of the combined impact of both the economic and non-economic measures contained in the proposed resolution.
