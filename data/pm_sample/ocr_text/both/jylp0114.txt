CORRELATION ANALYSIS OF ARSENIC, CADMIUM AND LEAD DETERMINED IN HAIR AND BLOOD OF NORMALLY CONTAMINATED PERSONS
F. Paulsen, C. Alsen-Hinrichs and S. Mai

Arsenic (As), cadmium (Cd) and lead (Pb) were determined in hair and blood samples obtained from 30 persons (18 women and 12 men, range of age: 19-78 years, 5 smokers and 25 non-smokers). Hairs were cut off from the neck and washed 5-times alternatively with aceton and aqua bidest., respectively, according to the method described by KRAUSE et al. (1987), Schr.R. WaBoLu 71, Fischer, Stuttgart. Blood and washed hair samples were wet ashed according to the method described by KOTZ et al. (1972), Z. Anal. Chem. 260, 207-209. Determinations of As, Cd and Pb were performed using well established methods of atomic absorption spectrometry. The results indicate a low level of metal contamination (blood -B - µg x liter; hair -H - µg x kg):

B-As: < 0.2-0.4; B-Cd: < 0.5-2.8; B-Pb: 20-102.
H-As: 3-24; H-Cd: 9-138; H-Pb: 110-7330.

Although the number of investigated persons is very low, the correlation analysis reveals some interesting results: H-As depending on B-As; H-Pb depending on B-Pb; H-Pb corresponding with H-Cd; H-Cd and H-Pb distal > proximal; H-As distal < proximal; H-Cd fair hair < dark hair; B-Cd and B-As and H-Pb smoker > non-smoker; B-Cd depending on number of cigarettes smoked per day; H-As and B-Pb and H-Pb men > women; drinking of wine increases B-Pb; B-Pb and drinking of mineral water are inversely correlated; B-Pb and consumption of vegetables are positively correlated; B-As and consumption of fish is positively correlated; B-Pb and H-Pb increasing values correlate with age of the houses the persons live in.

The results indicate that metal analyses of hair samples may be indicative for the degree of regional metal pollution and food consumption habits of humans. However, it should be kept in mind, that hair samples cannot so easily be handled as compared with blood samples.
