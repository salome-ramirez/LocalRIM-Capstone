FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Former New Mexico Governor Garrey Carruthers to Chair Non-Profit Group
NATIONAL WATCHDOG ORGANIZATION LAUNCHED TO
FIGHT UNSOUND SCIENCE USED FOR PUBLIC POLICY

(ALBUQUERQUE, N.M. -- November 18, 1993). In an address to members of the New Mexico Farm Bureau Convention today, former Governor of New Mexico Garrey Carruthers announced the formation of a new national grassroots organization, The Advancement of Sound Science Coalition (TASSC). The Farm Bureau Convention is the first stop in a national multi-city media tour to inform consumers and businesses of the role TASSC will play in monitoring unsound science.

"This group is long overdue," said Carruthers. "We believe that science used to guide public policy decisions should be based on sound principles -- not on emotions and beliefs considered by some as 'politically correct'. Too often, public policy decisions that are based on inadequate science impose enormous economic costs and other hardships on consumers, businesses and government. It’s time we expose this problem more vigilantly, and alert the public to the rapidly rising costs of regulations coming from these policies."

TASSC currently has over 300 members including scientists and representatives from universities and independent organizations and industry. Formed recently, TASSC has already obtained broad-based national support.

"Compliance with environmental regulations cost the nation an incredible $115 billion in 1991 alone. And we agree that government regulations are needed for enforcing legitimate guidelines," said Carruthers. "But as a former professor of agriculture economics, I am acutely aware of proper scientific guidelines -- guidelines written years ago and still valid today -- that are being cast aside to make science 'fit' with the political leanings of special interests. The cost to us is simply too high to see this disturbing trend continue."

To support the need for TASSC, Carruthers pointed to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency’s own report: "Safeguarding the Future: Credible Science, Credible Decisions," (March 1992), as acknowledging that their science is of "uneven quality".
