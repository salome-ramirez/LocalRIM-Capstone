RE: Draft letter to the editor, publication by Sun, Glantz et al
In their study, Sun et al1 investigated the question whether the β-adrenergic system is
involved in environmental tobacco smoke (ETS) induced etherosclerosis. Although not
mentioned in the article, this question is of vital importance to the validity of the animal
model used, because the involvement of the adrenergic system would suggest that the
model actually measures an artifact, i.e. the exposure related stress, and not a toxicological
property of ETS.
As a result of their study the authors conclude that "Metoproiol did not protect against the
effect of ETS on etherosclerosis, suggesting that the β -adrenergic system is not the
mechanism of ETS-induced etherosclerosis." However, the data presented in this
publication do not allow the authors to draw this conclusion; in fact, the data indicate that
the opposite conclusion is more likely to be true.
The authors report on a distinct reduction of arterial lesions in ETS exposed rabbits due to
the continuous infusion of Metoproiol. They characterize this as "slight...tendencies
(which) require some caution in interpreting our conclusion..." without discussing the
magnitude of this interaction of ETS and Metoproiol.
By the use of their evaluation model (general linear model) the authors could separate the
effect which was blockable by Metropoiol from the total effect. The article gives the
statistical significance of this "effect portion" but fails to report its magnitude. A more
informative presentation of their results with a split into the portions:
- Diet effect,
- ETS-independent, "unspecific" Metoproiol effect,
- Metropoiol-blockable ETS effect ("interaction"),

RE: Draft letter to the editor, publication by Sun, Glantz et al

In their study, Sun et al investigated the question whether the β-adrenergic system is involved in environmental tobacco smoke (ETS) induced etherosclerosis. Although not mentioned in the article, this question is of vital importance to the validity of the animal model used, because the involvement of the adrenergic system would suggest that the model actually measures an artifact, i.e. the exposure related stress, and not a toxicological property of ETS.

As a result of their study the authors conclude that "Metoproiol did not protect against the effect of ETS on etherosclerosis, suggesting that the β-adrenergic system is not the mechanism of ETS-induced etherosclerosis." However, the data presented in this publication do not allow the authors to draw this conclusion; in fact, the data indicate that the opposite conclusion is more likely to be true.

The authors report on a distinct reduction of arterial lesions in ETS exposed rabbits due to the continuous infusion of Metoproiol. They characterize this as "slight...tendencies (which) require some caution in interpreting our conclusion..." without discussing the magnitude of this interaction of ETS and Metoproiol.

By the use of their evaluation model (general linear model) the authors could separate the effect which was blockable by Metoproiol from the total effect. The article gives the statistical significance of this "effect portion" but fails to report its magnitude. A more informative presentation of their results with a split into the portions:

- Diet effect,
- ETS-independent, "unspecific" Metoproiol effect,
- Metoproiol-blockable ETS effect ("interaction"),
- Non-blockable residual ETS effect
