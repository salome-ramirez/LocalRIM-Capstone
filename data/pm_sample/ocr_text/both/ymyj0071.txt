America's Success Story

While other industries have suffered from foreign competition, tobacco exports have soared, providing a ray of hope for the American economy and a classic example of how to compete in the world marketplace.

It's no secret that the United States has become a debtor nation, spending more on imports from other countries than we sell in exports to the rest of the world. In 1987, the U.S. merchandise trade deficit grew to $171.2 billion compared to $156.2 billion in 1966.

After Japan opened its doors to American-made cigarettes, U.S. exports grew so rapidly that it required 747s to airship them to Japan.

Unlike some other industries, tobacco and tobacco products have been consistent winners in the foreign trade game. Tobacco provides a multi-billion dollar annual surplus to the national trade balance.

TOBACCO TRADE INCREASES

Just how important is the tobacco industry to U.S. foreign trade? Last year, Wharton Econometrics, an independent economic research firm, released a study demonstrating how tobacco and other agricultural product surpluses combat the U.S. trade deficit.

★ The value of tobacco leaf and product exports increased more than 25 percent to almost $3.4 billion in 1987, compared to $2.7 billion in 1966.

★ The tobacco industry posted a trade surplus of $2.7 billion in 1987, a 32 percent increase over 1986.
