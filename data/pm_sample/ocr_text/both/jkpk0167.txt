I am the 4-H Saipan (CNMI) Youth Leader. I have read your letter of 3/25/ 99
and just finished reviewing the recent Video presentations from the National 4-H
Program of which you are a partner. I have called your office and discussed some areas
with Nancy Zimbalist Supervisor for Consumer Affairs. I hope you will talk with her
after reading this letter thus covering most of my concerns.
My major concern is the focus of the program as it is presently directed. In both
your letter and the conversations by the students the National 4-H personnel and the
Philip Morris people I see no reference to the people who can create an environment to
move the program forward. This group of people is the 20 years of age up to at least the
age of 40. As these people are the Parents, Relatives, Neighbors, and the immediate
contacts for exemplary behavior of the youth they obviously can create the greatest
aware ess and change of attitude in the use of tobacco products, Not just Smoking.
I feel most of Society is concerned only with the Youth when in reality the most
influential and powerful people are in the age group mentioned as they are learning to
live through the every day struggles. This means personal disciplines which are passed
on to their families and create a bond of trust and love. When one becomes a parent
(single or married) we are not presented with a handbook How to be a Successful Parent
and a Guide to Solving Problems in Raising Children. If however we have strong values
based on good example and real knowledge we can provide love and understanding to our
growing children and siblings.
The age group I mentioned is basically confronted with the following statenent.
“You are old enough now to take care of yourself and handle any responsibility" anα then
left to their own devices right or wrong. I would much rather see the Churches the
Academic Community, The Social Workers, and Industry devote time and money to
make these people to quote the Army, "Be All That You Can Be". In fact the services are
the only agencies that live up to this criteria. They actually Train and Discipline the
youth for the future.

Dear Ms. Levy

I am the 4-H Saipan (CNMI) Youth Leader. I have read your letter of 3/25/99 and just finished reviewing the recent Video presentations from the National 4-H Program of which you are a partner. I have called your office and discussed some areas with Nancy Zimbalist Supervisor for Consumer Affairs. I hope you will talk with her after reading this letter thus covering most of my concerns.

My major concern is the focus of the program as it is presently directed. In both your letter and the conversations by the students the National 4-H personnel and the Philip Morris people I see no reference to the people who can create an environment to move the program forward. This group of people is the 20 years of age up to at least the age of 40. As these people are the Parents, Relatives, Neighbors, and the immediate contacts for exemplary behavior of the youth they obviously can create the greatest awareness and change of attitude in the use of tobacco products, Not just Smoking.

I feel most of Society is concerned only with the Youth when in reality the most influential and powerful people are in the age group mentioned as they are learning to live through the every day struggles. This means personal disciplines which are passed on to their families and create a bond of trust and love. When one becomes a parent (single or married) we are not presented with a handbook. How to be a Successful Parent and a Guide to Solving Problems in Raising Children. If however we have strong values based on good example and real knowledge we can provide love and understanding to our growing children and siblings.

The age group I mentioned is basically confronted with the following statement. "You are old enough now to take care of yourself and handle any responsibility" and then left to their own devices right or wrong. I would much rather see the Churches the Academic Community, The Social Workers, and Industry devote time and money to make these people to quote the Army, "Be All That You Can Be". In fact the services are the only agencies that live up to this criteria. They actually Train and Discipline the youth for the future.
