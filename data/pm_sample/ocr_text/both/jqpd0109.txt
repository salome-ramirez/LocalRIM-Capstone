Pittsburgh ‘light up’ project set

By Jeff Domenick
TRIBUNE-REVIEW

After the city of Pittsburgh's stringent ordinance on smoking in public places went in to effect last year, it created a lot of confusion — particularly for smokers.

Les Zuke of Philip Morris U.S.A said his cigarette manufacturing firm received telephone calls and letters from Pittsburgh smokers concerned about where they could “light up.”

The result of those contacts is a pilot program in the city, which if successful, Zuke said, could eventually spread throughout the country.

Known as the “Accommodation Program,” it will be an effort to have restaurants and businesses set up designated areas for smokers and non-smokers, its main thrust is to aid perplexed puffers.

“Most people are law-abiding citizens and will obey the law,” he said. “We want to help people understand the law and end confusion.”

The company has recruited 90 restaurants into the placard program. They range from fast-food places like Arby's in Oakland to elegant establishments like Ruth's Chris Steak House in Downtown.

One hotel, the Sheraton at Sta-tion Square has also been enlisted, providing non-smoking rooms and separate areas in the lobby.

Two businesses are involved thus far, the Prudential-Bache investment firm on the 27th floor of the U.S. Steel Building and the Yellow Cab Co.

Prudential-Bache branch manager Jack Hutchison, who noted he likes “a good cigar once in a while,” said among the steps the firm has taken are separate lunch rooms and conference rooms for its 70 employees.

“It’s been positive for us and hasn’t been hard for anyone,” Hutchison said. “It’s a lot easier than just telling people they can’t smoke.”

At Yellow Cab, drivers will be able to put the smoker/non-smoker stickers on their cabs, Zuke said.

He said area residents will begin seeing the logo, designed by the Burston-Marsteller firm of Pittsburgh, around the city this week. Also planned is a three-month advertising effort to make people aware of the program and seek other businesses to join.
