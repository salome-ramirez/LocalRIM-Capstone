FDA CONSIDERS EASING OFF-LABEL USE REQUIREMENTS FOR EXPERIMENTAL DRUGS

Top FDA officials are in the "discussion stages" of crafting a policy that would ease restrictions on the dissemination of information for off-label uses of drugs to treat cancer, AIDS and possibly other life-threatening diseases, according to key agency sources. The current discussions are part of a broader initiative at the agency to provide cancer patients greater access to experimental drugs. FDA is holding the internal talks at a time when Sen. Nancy Kassebaum (R-KS) is proposing to expedite approvals of these drugs as part of her FDA reform proposal, and some sources say the agency's initiative could be an attempt by FDA to preempt the lawmaker's reform plans.

FDA is considering "a number of initiatives" proposed by a wide-range of sources inside and outside the agency, continued on page 4

HOUSE PANEL: KESSLER MAY HAVE VIOLATED COURT ORDER ON PEDICLE SCREW

A key House subcommittee has accused FDA Commissioner David Kessler of possibly violating a court order in his handling of court-sealed documents involving pedicle screw uses. The case in question involves a consolidated product liability suit in which FDA had mistakenly received court-sealed documents. The Republican lawmaker is attempting to determine to what extent Kessler reviewed the documents and was aware that the information was subject to a court order. Moreover, the congressman is questioning why the documents were not immediately returned, as FDA itself has argued is the appropriate action for mistakenly receiving court-sealed information.

FDA EYES ELA AS FOUNDATION FOR UNIQUE REG PLAN FOR TISSUE PRODUCTS
