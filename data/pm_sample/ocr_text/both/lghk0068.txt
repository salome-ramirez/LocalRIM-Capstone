INTRODUCTION
In an effort to assist Philip Morris Management Corporation (“Philip Morris” or “the
Company") with the monitoring and analysis of the World Health Organization's (“WHO")
proposed Framework Convention on Tobacco Control, we have described below the typical
procedure for making a modern “multilateral agreement” (also referred to in international
parlance as a(n) “accord,” “treaty," “convention,” or “international agreement”) from two
perspectives. The first is a "multi-country" or “international” perspective whereby government
representatives meet under the auspices of an international organization (e.g., the World Health
Organization) or in connection with an existing treaty to negotiate a new or amended multilateral
agreement. (See Chart I, attached). The second is a “negotiating country” or “domestic"
perspective whereby a negotiating country engages in the steps necessary to authorize
negotiation, signature and, if possible, a binding commitment to implement the multilateral
agreement under the provisions of its legal system. For this second perspective we describe the
typical steps in making a modern multilateral agreement from the perspective of the United
States. (See Chart II, attached).
It is worth noting here that a "framework convention" (e.g., the proposed Framework
Convention on Tobacco Control) refers to an existing, broad-scope multilateral agreement
negotiated under the auspices of an international organization (e.g., the WHO). Whereas a
"protocol" (e.g., a possible Protocol on the Prevention of Tobacco Smoking Among Children)
generally refers to an accord that supplements, is more specific in scope than and is negotiated
pursuant to an existing multilateral agreement.
Generally speaking, four steps are involved in the making of multilateral agreements:
1) negotiation; 2) adoption; 3) signature and ratification; and 4) entry into force. These steps are
described below from each perspective.
Additionally, this memorandum identifies, if and where appropriate, advocacy 2065346732
opportunities that can effect or avert changes in domestic, regional, or international policy and
law related to the prospective multilateral agreement. These opportunities are most evident
during the negotiation and signature and ratification steps in the making of a modern multilateral
agreement and enable the Company to strategically plan for involvement in decisionmaking in
appropriate fora.
