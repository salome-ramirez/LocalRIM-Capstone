Subject: : MARLBORO RKS TEST MARKETS
Marlboro with 2.5% RKS has been in the Columbus and Atlanta warehouse areas for
almost 1 year. At this date there is no evidence that the change has been detected
by smokers in those areas and no unfavorable sales results have been found in the
test markets.
Based on the test market results, there seems to be no objection to incorporating
2.5% RKS in al1 production Marlboro.
Discussion
This is a further analysis of the data quoted in a previous memo (R. Saleeby, 11/29/66)
on this subject. At that time there was some evidence that some of the control and
test markets were unsuitable (i.e. that they were responding to influences not in the
scope of the test). In order to ascertain the suitability of various possible control
markets, an Analysis of Variance procedure-was applied on the relative performances
of the tests and possible control markets previous to the test date.
In the case of the Atlanta warehouse test market it was found that Alabama and South
Carolina figures had varied too widely from the test market in periods prior to the
test to be used as controls. The extreme fluctuations in South Carolina were found
to have been caused mainly by fluctuations in the, considerable military industry in
that state. The balance of Florida was substituted for the two states mentioned above
and the state of Tennessee was: retained as the second control.
The difference in Columbus between the central market and the balance of warehouse
area was found to stem from one direct account who apparently left the market during
the test period. After adjustment had been made for this account, there were no serious
discrepancies between the Columbus market and blance of warehouse area. It was found
that Cincinnati and Indiana were the most suitable control markets for the Columbus
warehouse area but, even with these two markets Marlboro sales in the Columbus warehouse
show a slightly inferior trend prior to the test period.
The data displayed in the appendix table are compari'sons of percent changes for the
tests versus controls over a common base. The Marlboro data are adjusted by similar
data for Parliament in order to rule out any local situations which would affect the
markets unequally.

figures. Since we are most interested in possible negative test results only the negative
limits are listed. The following table is intended as an aid in reading the appendix
data. The "Adjusted Advantage for Marlboro" is to be considered as significantly lower
than zero only if it is more negative than a given significance level will allow. The

chosen, the greater the degree of certainty.
