Here are my suggestions re: WHO treaty response (ME Counts)
Other Smoke Constituents:
“In addition to tar and nicotine yield disclosures, we support legislation enabling
governments to require disclosure of information about other individual constituents in
mainstream cigarette smoke, provided the information can be generated using
standardized and commercially feasible test methods or reliably predicted from test data.
Tesiing procedures and analytical methods must be scientifically validated and should
be recognized by international standard-setting organizations.
The reporting of individual cigarette mainstream smoke constituent yields has been
discussed by tobacco manufacturers, public health communities, and/or regulatory
bodies in a number of countries. Through considerable research in smoke constituent
yields for conventional cigarette brands, it has been demonstrated that many smoke
constituent yields are highly correlated with “tar’ and nicotine yields. When a subset of
brand styles, with cigarette design features encompassing those of the relevant market
place, is tested for individual smoke constituents, mathematical relationships between
those smoke constituents and the yields of “tar” and nicotine are developed. These
relationships are then used to predict smoke constituent yields for other brand styles
with similar design features from the “tar” or nicotine yields of those brand styles. This
approach of using data from a subset of brands to predict yields for other brands is
termed “benchmarking.” The mathematical relationships developed from a
benchmarking study would allow for both a predicted mean and range for individual
smoke constituent yields. The relationships apply, however, only to brand styles of
manufacturers represented in the brand style subset. Whether smoke constituent
yields are explicitly tested or predicted from robust relationships, it must be recognized
that there are no international standard analytical methods for these smoke constituents.
It is expected that, lacking such standard methods, there will be inherent variation in
smoke yield data determined by different laboratories.
