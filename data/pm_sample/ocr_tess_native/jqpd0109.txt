GREENSBURG TRIBUNE REVIEW
June 2, 1989
Pittsburgh ‘light up’ project set
By Jeff Domenick
TRIBUNE-REVIEW
After the city of Pittsburgh’s
stringent ordinance on smoking in
public places went in to effect last
year, it created a lot of confusion
— particularly for smokers.
Les Zuke of Philip Morris U.S.A
said his cigarette manufacturing
firm received telephone calls and
letters from Pittsburgh smokers
concerned about where they
could “light up.”
The result of those contacts is a
pilot program in the city, which if
successful, Zuke said, could even-
tually spread throughout the
country.
Known as the “Accommodation
Program,” it will be an effort to
have restaurants and businesses
set up designated areas for
smokers and non-smokers alike
and install placards at their en-
trances announcing the facilities
are available.
The New York-based Philip
Morris, which makes Marlboro
brand of cigarettes and many
others, has commissioned the
placards. They have a red and a
green swirl in a circular pattern,
with a burning cigarette logo in
the green swirl.
“The symbol works for both
customers and business owners,’
said Zuke, the company’s director
of public relations. “It gives
owners a positive way to commu-
nicate that their customers’ pre-
ferences will be met. And it allows
customers to easily identify that
message.”
The announcement of the pro-
gram was made Thursday at a
news conference at the Pittsburgh
Press Club.
Philip Morris spokesman
Steven Weiss said although the
accommodation program benefits
smokers and non-smokers, its
main thrust is to aid perplexed
puffers.
“Most people are law-abiding
citizens and will obey the law,” he
said. ‘‘We want to help people un-
derstand the law and end confu-
sion.”
The company has recruited 90
restaurants into the placard pro-
gram. They range from fast-food
places like Arby’s in Oakland to
elegant establishments like
Ruth’s Chris Steak House in
Downtown.
One hotel, the Sheraton at Sta-
tion’ Square has also been en-
listed, providing non-smoking
rooms and separate areas in the
lobby.
Two businesses are involved
thus far, the Prudential-Bache in-
vestment firm on the 27th floor of
the U.S. Steel Building and the
Yellow Cab Co.
Prudential-Bache branch man-
ager Jack Hutchison, who noted
he likes ‘‘a good cigar once in a |
while,” said among the steps the
firm has taken are separate lunch
rooms and conference rooms for
its 70 employees.
“Tt’s been positive for us and
hasn’t been hard for anyone,”
Hutchison said. “It’s a lot easier
than just telling people they can’t ;
smoke.”
At Yellow Cab, drivers will be
able to put the smoker/non-
smoker stickers on their cabs,
Zuke said.
He said area residents will be-
gin seeing the logo, designed by
the Burston-Marsteller firm of
Pittsburgh, around the city this
week. Also planned is a three-
month advertising effort to make
people aware of the program and
seek other businesses to join.
