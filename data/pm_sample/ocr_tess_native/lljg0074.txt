Tucker P., A Numerical Study of Optimal Room Layouts and Devices to
Minimize Passive Smoking
Description of the Project::
Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) has become an important tool to assess the
performance of ventilation systems, i.e., air flows within rooms and contaminant
removal from an indoor space. Computer simulations can be done for virtually any
situation by making a number of reasonable assumptions, with a typical objective
being to maximize system performance in order to minimize exposure to some
contaminants of interest. Validation and certification of CFD tools is still an ongoing
process and Dr Tucker’s work will help to address the critical questions of quality and
trust of CFD results.
Dr Tucker’s work should increase our understanding of smoke distribution in rooms.
On that basis, CFD should provide an tool that can be used to minimize exposure to
ETS.
Evaluation against Philip Morris External Research Agenda:
la. Exposure/Biomarkers/Dosimetry, Measures of exposure
This project is designed to provide Philip Morris with much improved methodology to
assess the distribution of ETS in any given venue and, as a consequence, determine
the effectiveness of any given ventilation strategy in minimizing the expsosure of
non-smokers to ETS. As a consequence, it clearly fits in with the external research
objective of determining exposure. It is important to note that Philip Morris’ current
interest in exposure tends to involve the exposure of the smoker himself or herself
with respect to either whole smoke or some component of interest. However,
exposure of the non-smoker should not be neglected. Harm reduction is as
important with respect to the non-smoker as it is with respect to the smoker, despite
the fact that there is little evidence to support many of the putative effects of ETS on
the non-smoker. Methodologies to evaluate the effectiveness of ETS remediation,
therefore, are an important aspect of the PM program.
