ERNEST VAN DEN. HAAG
“In a democracy, legislation against activities desired by a substantial number
of citizens: is ineffective," says Ernest van deni Haag, Ph.D., professor of
jurisprudence and public policy at Fordham University's Law School.
While van den Haag was specifically referring to the rights. of smokers to smoke,
he notes that precedents have been set in other areas and in other times
throughout American history, citing the well-known example of Prohibition.
"Smokers have: the right to indulge where and when they wish as. long as there is
no law restricting smoking," he added.
Van den Haag's distinguished career has included an adjunct professorship in law
at the New York Law School and the position of lecturer of sociological studies
at the New School for Social Research. His observations in the relationship.
between the law and smokers' rights reflect his own background studies in both
jurisprudence, psychology and sociology. He has also been published in these
two areas in both professional and consumer books and magazines.
