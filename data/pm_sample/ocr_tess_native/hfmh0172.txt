1996 INDIANAPOLIS 500 -- SITUATION ANALYSIS
Basically, the problem is that Indianapolis Motor Speedway president, Tony George, wants to
take control of Indy car racing away from Championship Auto Racing Teams, Inc. (CART), the
association of Team Owners. Through organizations which he controls absolutely, he plans to
become the sanctioning body, the promoter, the rulemaker and the arbiter. Whether, in fact, this
is good for the sport is a question anyone who has dealt with the establishment which runs the
indianapolis 500 will have no difficulty in answering.
The facts are relatively simple. George is not happy that under the management of CART, Indy
car racing has become an international series with strong foreign competition on a wide variety of
road courses and street circuits, many of which represent key markets for teams and sponsors.
Instead he plans to create a series for oval tracks only, and is trying to regulate that series back
to the low budget, low tech, all American “backyard-engineering’ era of the 1940s and ‘50s --
ignoring the fact that cars built and maintained by such part time Owner-Driver-Mechanics will
certainly be far more dangerous and less reliable than contemporary professionally engineered
and maintained vehicles. The facts are:
1. In order to encourage participation in the initial five race Indy Racing League (JRL) series,
set up to rival the established 15 race CART PPG Indy Car World Series, George has
reserved 25 of the 33 starting positions in the 1996 Indianapolis 500 for entries with points
from IRL races. In other words, unless you compete in the early season IRL races, only
eight spots are open in the Indianapolis 500 field.
2. 1996 IRL races (including the Indianapolis 500) are open only to cars built to 1995 Indy
car rules. These do not include a number of important driver safety improvements
mandated by 1996 CART rules. Moreover, 1996 CART regulations have substantially
reduced downforce in the interests of safety. ironically, the 1996 CART rules were drafted
in accordance with principles originally recommended by the Indianapolis Motor
Speedway with the full knowledge and agreement of Mike Devin, Technical Director for
the Indianapolis 500.
3. For 1997, proposed IRL rules obsolete all current Indy car engines and will effectively
exclude Ford, Honda, Mercedes and Toyota from the series. The new IRL engine formula
requires a four liter V8 production based block, not turbocharged, and running a 10,500
rom rev limiter.
. Not only are such engines inherently fragile (design priorities for road-going
production engines are vastly different), but also, unless a manufacturer just
happens to have a V8 production engine “freely on sale for retail purchasers” with
the right displacement, bore centers, block deck height and cam/valve
arrangement, they will be seriously discouraged from becoming involved.
° Like all modern single seater racing cars, current Indy cars use the engine as a
stressed part of the chassis structure. This is not a criteria for production engine
designs so alternative chassis strengthening will be required. Since no serious
formula has used such structures for over 20 years, this reverses the painstaking
design evolution leading to the relative safety enjoyed by the current generation of
Indy cars.
