PHILIP MORRIS APPLAUDS DISMISSAL OF PENNSYLVANIA
CLASS ACTION CASE
NEW YORK, October 17, 1997 -- Philip Morris U.S.A. applauds today’s decision by a
federal judge in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, dismissing the Barnes class-action case
that was scheduled for trial in November.
"This decision is a major victory for the tobacco industry," said John J. Mulderig,
associate general counsel for Philip Morris. “It sends a clear message across the
country that class actions just do not work in tobacco cases because they inevitably
turn on individual issues of fact and law.”
In separate opinions, U.S. District Judge Clarence Newcomer decertified the Barnes
class and granted judgment in favor of the defendants on the individual plaintiffs’
claims.
Today's decision is consistent with last year's landmark decision by the Fifth U.S.
Circuit Court of Appeals in the nationwide class-action Castano case. Since that
decision, plaintiffs’ lawyers have filed approximately 38 cases purportedly on behalf of
statewide classes.
"The Barnes case was the first of these so-called post-Castano purported class actions
scheduled to go to trial, and we are delighted that this federal court has ruled that the
case has no legal merit," Mulderig said.
