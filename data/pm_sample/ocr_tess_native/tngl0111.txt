Application 1 iis the patch filter which has carbon applied to the plugwrap
axound a more ventional filter. The carbon is applied in rectangular areas
with gaps between successive areas. The advantage is that all cutting can be
done in the clean area thus avoiding dust and wear on knives. From the product
viewpoint there is the possibility of changing the balance of vapor phase
components in the smokestream by interaction with the activated carbon while
the conventional delivery of the product is controlled by the conventional
filter. This further simplifies construction by only requiring a mono filter.
An optimum application of this would semm to be a design where a concentric
filter with a very high RTD core is used to force the smokestream flow to be
close to the activated charcoal.
Application 2 involves the FIL Activated Carbon Thread filter (ACT). This
filter has activated carbon attached to threads that run down the center of
the filter and are wrapped with either paper or CA. By making this coarse
carbon there is very little resistance to flow in the center region and so the
smoke flow passes preferentially around the carbon. In a product this allows
control of the balance, especially the vapor-phase components, with minimal
effect on the total delivery. The delivery and the effective RTD, as seen by
the smoker, are controlled by a second filter element that is placed
downstream of the carbon element, thus giving the appearance of a conventional
product. BAT has already moved in the area since their patent EP 0 579 410 Al
seems designed to get around the use of the Filtrona filter patents.
