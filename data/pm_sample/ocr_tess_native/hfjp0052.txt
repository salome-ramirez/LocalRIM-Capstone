COMMENTS CONCERNING SMOKING IN AIRPORTS
1, PATH ONLY: DOT has suggested that guaranteeing passengers sensitive to tobacco smoke “a path of access from the terminal
entrance to the aircraft free from exposure to tobacco smoke,” perbaps by using “an enclosed cart that took the passenger through
the airport without exposure to smoke," would provide sufficient protection. Do you agree or disagree? Why? PRINT
This is a ridiculous compromise--enclosed carts are expensive--all human
beings deserve to be able to breathe clean, fresh air. Smoking should
not be allowed to pollute indoor air anywhere in the airport facility.
Ambient air carries the pollutants to all parts; it is just a fiction
to believe that when some indoor air is polluted, the rest is not.
2, OTHER AREAS: Are there other areas of the airport terminal that passengers — including those sensitive to tobacco smoke
— frequently must use while traveling, and where they therefore need protection from exposure to tobacco smoke (¢.g., restrooms,
telephones, eating areas, baggage claim, news stands and other stores, ticket counters, etc.)? If yes, explain why these areas are
important to travelers, and what smoking restrictions (if any) should be adopted. PRINT
In the Minneapolis-St. Paul Airport, where I live, indoor smoking is
not allowed. This is as it should be. All areas used by the public
should be protected from air polluted by cigarette/cigar smoking.
