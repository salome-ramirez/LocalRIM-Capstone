CORRELATION ANALYSIS OF ARSENIC, CADMIUM AND LEAD DETERMINED
IN HAIR AND ELODD OF NORMALLY CONTAMINATED PERSONS
F, Paulsen, C. Alsen-Hinrichs and S. Mai
Arsenic (As), cadmium (Cd) and lead (Pb) were determined in
hair and blood samples obtained from 30 persons (18 women and
12 men, range of age: 19-78 years, 5 smokers and 25 non-smo-
kers). Hairs were cut off from the neck and washed S-times al-
ternatively with aceton and aqua bidest., respectively, ac-
cording to the method described by KRAUSE et al. (1987),
Schr.R. WaBoLu 71, Fischer, Stuttgart. Blood and washed hair
samples were wet ashed according to the method described by
KOTZ et al. (1972), Z. Anal. Chem. 260, 207-209. Determina-
tions of As, Cd and Pb were performed using well established
methods of atomic absorption spectrometry. The results indi-
cate a,low level of metal coptamination (blood -B - yg x
liter; hair -K - yg x kg )
B-As: < 0.2-0.4; B-Cd: < 0.5-2.8: B-Pb: 20-102.
H-As: 3-24; H-Cd: 9-138; H-Pb: 110-7330.
Although the number of investigated persons is very low, the
correlation analysis reveales some interesting results: H-As
depending on B-As; H-Pb depending on B-Pb; H-Pb corresponding
with H-Cd; H-Cd and H-Pb distal > proximal; H-As distal <
proximal; H-Cd fair hair < dark hair; B-Cd and B-As and H-Pb
smoker > non-smoker; B-Cd depending on number of cigarettes
smoked per day; H-As and B-Pb and H-Pb men > women; drinking
of wine increases B-Pb; B-Pb and drinking of mineral water are
inversely correlated; B-Pb and consumption of vegetables are
positively correlated; B-As and consumption of fish is positi-
vely correlated; B-Pb and H-Pb increasing values correlate
with age of the houses the persons live in.
The results indicate that metal analyses of hair samples may
be indicative for the degree of regional metal pollution and
food consumption habits of humans. However, ft should be kept
in mind, that hair samples cannot so easily be handled as
compared with blood samples.
