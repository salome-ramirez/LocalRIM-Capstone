Thank you for your letter of 15th March concerning the two official reports on the

In response to the official letter please file the enclosed set of claims.
Please respond to the Examiner with the following arguments:
The process disclosed by US 4253473 (Hibbitts) does not include the step of cooling
the tobacco before the contacting step, which is step e) of the present invention. Hibbitts discloses
only that the internal temperature of the pressure vessel during spray impregnation will vary from
about -8°F to about 56°F (see column 3, lines 29-37). Hibbitts does not teach or suggest the step
of cooling the tobacco before the impregnation step.
Hibbitts uses a sparge tube to spray liquid carbon dioxide onto tobacco instead of
immersing the tobacco in a tub of liquid carbon dioxide as taught in U.S. 1,789,435 (see column 1
of Hibbitts). In doing so Hibbitts teaches to expand tobacco “by means of liquid CO, in a simple,
efficient and highly economical manner" (Hibbitts column 1, lines 66-68; see also column 2, lines
43-46 and 63-65, column 3, lines 6-8). Thus Hibbitts teaches a more efficient way to apply liquid
carbon dioxide over prior immersion practices. The mixture of liquid and gaseous CO, to which
the tobacco is contacted is introduced into the vessel by spraying liquid CO, (see column 3, line
14). However, in our process gaseous CO, is introduced into the vessel. The use of gaseous rather
than liquid CO, in the present invention leads to a much lower energy requirement in the present
invention. Also, our process provides a more uniform application of CO, throughout the tobacco
bed than a sprayed mist of liquid carbon dioxide, as in Hibbitts, would provide.
