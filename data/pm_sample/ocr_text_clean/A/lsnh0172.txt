TOBACCO: LITIGATION EVENT TIMELINE: JANUARY UPDATE
North America: United States of America
* CONTINUE TO BELIEVE THAT LEGAL RISK PROFILE HAS IMPROVED Other
than U.S. Supreme Court FDA oral arguments the Court seemed highly skeptical of the
agency's position there were relatively few tobacco legal developments during December.
* THE 3 KEY REMAINING CHALLENGES SHOULD BE RESOLVED IN 2000 These
include Engle's punitive damage structure, the status of FDA tobacco regulator authority, and
the ruling on the industry's initial attempt to dismiss the DOJ's tobacco lawsuit.
* ADDITIONAL EVIDENCE THAT THE DOJ'S SUIT IS POLITICALLY DRIVEN
The Clinton Administration's intent to sue gun manufacturers, in our opinion, provides
additional evidence that its tobacco assault is a politically motivated suit not based on the
law.
* ENGLE REMAINS THE MARKET'S KEY CONCERN Given the Court's extraordinary
response to the industry's motion, we remain reasonably optimistic that the Court will bar a
lump sum punitive damage award.
Litigation Event Timeline: January Update
Investment summary and conclusion:
Key Near- and Intermediate-Term Legal and Regulatory Developments (in order of likely
occurrence):
(1) The Florida Supreme Court's decision on the constitutionality of a lump sum punitive
damage award in the Florida Engle class action claim.
(2) A ruling on the industry's motion to dismiss the Guatemalan healthcare recovery lawsuit.
(3) The jury verdict in the first individual smoker claim in a liberal jurisdiction -- Whiteley --
since the Henley and Williams-Branch losses.
(4) Rulings by the Fifth (argued on November 3, 1999), Eighth and Eleventh Circuits regarding
industry motions to dismiss third-party union claims.
(5) Judge Gladys Kessler's ruling on the industry's motion to dismiss the three union claims
before her.
