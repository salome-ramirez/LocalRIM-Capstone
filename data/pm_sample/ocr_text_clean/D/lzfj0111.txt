INVESTOR ADVICE

Philip Morris profits up 16%

REUTERS

NEW YORK - Philip Morris Cos. Inc. reported a 16 percent increase in third-quarter profit Thursday, boosted by gains in its international businesses.

The food and tobacco conglomerate said it earned $1.29 billion, or $1.44 a share, compared with $1.11 billion, or $1.20 a share, a year earlier.

Revenues were $15.0 billion, up from $13.77 billion.

But the results failed to impress Wall Street, where Philip Morris stock fell $3.375 a share to $80.625 on the New York Stock Exchange in early trading.

"Given very difficult market conditions, and the recent growth of discount products in several of our categories, we are pleased with our third-quarter performance, particularly in our international business," said Michael Miles, Philip Morris' chairman and chief executive, in a statement.

International sales jumped 22.9 percent in the latest quarter, with tobacco sales rising 20.8 percent to $3.64 billion and revenues from food products up 26.7 percent to 2.12 billion.

By contrast, tobacco sales in the United States rose much more slowly, gaining 5.43 percent to $2.98 billion, and North American food sales rose just 2 percent, to $5.09 billion.

THE WALL STREET JOURNAL FRIDAY, OCTOBER 16, 1992

Philip Morris 3rd-Period Net Hurts Its Stock

By SUEIN L. HWANG

Staff Reporter of THE WALL STREET JOURNAL

NEW YORK — Philip Morris Cos. posted a less-than-expected 16% rise in third-quarter earnings in the clearest indication to date that the nation’s No. 1 tobacco maker has run into trouble.

The tobacco and food giant said third-quarter net was $1.29 billion, or $1.44 a share, compared with $1.11 billion, or $1.20 a share, in the year-earlier period. Per share net jumped 20%, reflecting the company’s repurchase of 7.6 million shares for $593 million in the quarter. Revenue rose 9% to $15 billion from $13.77 billion.

The results fell short of Philip Morris’s typical 20% quarterly earnings gain. But what alarmed investors far more is the mounting evidence that the U.S. tobacco unit, the company’s largest and most profitable business, has been badly hurt by the rush to cheaper generic and subgeneric cigarette brands. Third-quarter operating profit for the U.S. tobacco business rose 6.5% to $1.3 billion, far below the 15% gains analysts had grown to expect.

On news of the earnings, most analysts slashed their earnings estimates for the fourth quarter and 1993. Philip Morris shares lost 5% of their value yesterday, falling $1.25 to $79.75, down $4.25, in very heavy New York Stock Exchange composition. Analysts and company insiders believe Philip Morris’s woes have been compounded by a seriously flawed sales strategy. In recent months, the company flooded retail stores with its high-margin, full-price brands, particularly top-selling Mariboro. But with that move, top brass at the tobacco unit, including recently appointed senior vice president of sales Michael Szymanczyk, underestimated magnitude of smokers’ defection to account brands, company insiders say. Unit sales of Mariboro, Philip Morris’s biggest brand by far, sank 3.9% to 30.6 billion in the third quarter.

They felt they had to push more inventory out there so their shelf space equaled their consumption levels,” said Rebecca Barfield, analyst at First Boston. “They didn’t realize it would be this big a shift to discount brands.”

In addition, analysts speculated that Philip Morris may be experiencing fallout from archival RJR Nabisco Holding Corp.’s recent efforts to revive its premium brands, most notably Camel “RJR Nabisco is succeeding in stabilizing its market share,” said Roy Burry, analyst at Kudder Peabody. “They’re less of a whipping boy than they used to be.”

A Philip Morris spokesman said the company has been focusing on the profitable, premium category. But while he conceded the marketplace has shifted away from premium brands, he stressed that Philip Morris’s share of the category is increasing. Nonetheless, because retailers are overloaded with its full price cigarettes, Philip Morris predicted that its fourth quarter U.S. tobacco shipments will drop 10%.

Overseas, Philip Morris’s international tobacco business lared better, operating profit increased 197 to $589 million, due in large part to favorable currency rates.
