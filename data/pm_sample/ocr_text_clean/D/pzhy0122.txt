Anheuser-Busch Companies, Inc.: Attractive

■ ANHEUSER-BUSCH MAKES LARGE INVESTMENT IN MEXICAN BREWER

Recent moves by BUD indicate something of an intensification in the company's focus on the international beer business. The company announced that it plans to purchase an 18% stake in Mexico's largest brewer, Grupo Modelo (the manufacturer of Corona), for $477 million, with options to increase its investment to approximately 35%, exerciseable between mid-1995 and the end of 1997. The purchase is not expected to be dilutive.

These announcements come on the heels of BUD's moves in the United Kingdom whereby BUD will be responsible for all marketing, sales, and distribution of its products in the United Kingdom, just as it will be in Japan. On the European Continent, BUD continues its negotiations with Budvar, the Government-owned brewer in Czechoslovakia. Budvar has the rights to the Budweiser name on the European Continent, and, in fact, has its own Budweiser beer, a product quite different from Anheuser's Budweiser (darker, etc.). We believe that BUD is interested in purchasing a minority-sized position in Budvar. The agreement would involve BUD in the marketing of both Budweisers, possibly under some sort of royalty arrangement with Budvar. It would not be at all surprising to see an agreement between Anheuser-Busch and Budvar entered into this year.

What all of this appears to indicate is that BUD's first priority after the domestic beer business is the international beer business—more than snack foods, entertainment (Busch Gardens and Sea World), baked goods, or the metal container business. This is not to say that the other lines of business will not remain important to the company, but rather that when all is said and done, the beer business outside of the U.S. is most likely to be the additional vehicle adding to the company's per share earnings on a meaningful basis over the longer term.

On a separate, albeit related, note, we would point out that BUD's international moves are coming at the same time that Miller is adopting a more aggressive posture towards the beer business outside of the U.S. Specifically, in recent months Philip Morris¹ (MO, $64) has bought 20% of Molson Breweries in Canada and acquired a nearly 8% share of Femaa, Mexico's other large brewer. It would not be at all surprising to see MO increase its stake in Femaa at some point in the not too distant future.

Indeed, we would look for Miller to make further investments in the international beer business, concurrent with comparable moves by BUD. In a nutshell, we have seen serious moves by both BUD and MO to become major forces in the international beer business, and we view these moves as just the beginning of a series of investments by each company during the remainder of the 1990's.
