Tracy still has pole spot

BY MARY BARKER

Herald Sports Writer

Jacques Villeneuve tried to avoid the wall.
Nigel Mansell tried to keep his balance.
Paul Tracy didn't bother.
He didn't have to. Tracy's Friday record qualifying time of 70.058 (113.768 miles per hour) held up through yesterday's session, giving him the pole for today's 1 p.m. Indy Car competition at Laguna Seca Raceway, the final event of the 1994 season.
Tracy earned a $10,000 bonus from Marlboro as part of the Marlboro Pole Award program. And if he wins today's race, he will get an additional $75,000.
Actually, the top three Friday times are still in place today after drivers battled 90-degree temperatures and precarious track conditions yesterday.
"It literally is like a skating rink out there at the moment," said Mansell, who qualified third in 71.071 (112.146 mph) for his final run with the Newman-Haas team. "The biggest feat was staying on the circuit."
Ask Villeneuve.
He had trouble coming out of Turn 2, as one of his tires left the track.
"The car was bottoming out," said Villeneuve, the second-place qualifier in 71.010 (112.244 mph). "I tried to go too quick and I went a little wide."
Tracy, meanwhile, was watching all of this from the pits.
After "flat-spotting" a set of tires in the morning practice — which amounts to losing rubber and ruining the grip — Tracy decided simply to use the qualifying session to "scrub" a set of fresh tires for today's race. He completed a partial lap.

"We really tried to do our best time the first day," he said. "It's good because it was quite a bit hotter today."

Should that trend continue as expected this afternoon, all three drivers said the race could get interesting.

"It's going to be tough to keep the tires on the car," said Tracy, who needs to finish sixth or better to end the season third in the points standings behind already-determined champion Al Unser Jr. and runner-up Emerson Fittipaldi. "It's a long race and this track is always hard on tires."

Mansell will be equally concerned with equilibrium.

"Our car doesn't quite have the balance of the Penske," he said.
