The following statement was issued today by Lewis E. January, M.D., president of the American Heart Association:

"The American Heart Association has been deeply involved with the problems of smoking and health for many years. Long before the matter received official consideration by the Federal Communications Commission, the Association had supplied all communications media, including broadcasters, with educational materials warning of the harmful effects of smoking.

"Following the FCC ruling in June, the Heart Association promptly sent an official communication to FCC Chairman Rosel Hyde indicating its great concern about the effects of cigarette smoking on the incidence of coronary heart disease, and stating that it was accelerating its efforts to provide materials to all radio and TV stations.

The Association also urgently requested all affiliated Heart Associations throughout the country to step up their educational campaigns in line with the action of the FCC. Furthermore, as a member of the Inter-Agency Council on Smoking and Health, the American Heart Association endorsed a letter from the Agency's chairman, Dr. Luther Terry, addressed to Chairman Hyde, which supported the FCC stand and promised fullest co-operation."
