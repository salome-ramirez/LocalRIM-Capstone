Liggett Group Settles With States Suing Cigarette Makers
They Get A Piece Of Profits For 25 Years, Won’t Thwart LeBow’s RJR Strategy

WASHINGTON (Reuter) — Liggett Group and five states suing the tobacco industry to recoup health care costs of smokers reached a multi-million dollar settlement Friday, representatives of both groups said.

Under terms of the settlement, Liggett will make an immediate cash payment to the states and continue to pay a share of its income for the next 24 years, while the states will drop Liggett as a defendant in the lawsuit.

The states also agreed not to seek legal restraint against a spinoff by RJR Nabisco Holdings Inc. of its tobacco businesses, a victory for investor Bennett Lebow, who has been trying to win control of the business through a proxy battle. Lebow is chairman of the parent company of Liggett, Brooke Group Ltd.

Under the terms of the settlement, Liggett will make an immediate cash payment of $1 million and an additional $4 million over the next 10 years.

Liggett also agreed to pay 2.5% to 7.5% of its pretax income, depending on the number of states that join the settlement, but a minimum of $30 million a year.

Liggett reached agreement in the case with the attorneys general of Florida, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Mississippi and West Virginia. As a result, Liggett and Brooke Group will be released from all tobacco-related Medicaid reimbursement claims by the states.

The agreement comes two days after Lebow stunned the tobacco industry when he broke ranks with a previously united front defending the industry against all challenges.

Liggett said Wednesday it had reached a tentative settlement to resolve its liability in a major class action suit pending in New Orleans federal court brought on behalf of smokers claiming to be addicted to cigarettes.

As with the prior settlement, terms of the new agreement would extend to another company if it were to merge with Liggett, a strategy pursued by Lebow to help win support from RJR shareholders for his proxy battle.

The proxy battle is likely to come to a head April 17 when RJR stockholders are scheduled to vote on a proposal put forth by Lebow to replace RJR’s directors with his own slate.

Brooke said if the spinoff were completed and RJR joined Liggett in the settlement, RJR would pay “a little more than a penny a pack.”

If another company were to merge with Liggett, the five states would share an additional $135 million, and other states joining the settlement would share another $25 million, the Brooke Group said in a news release.

Another term of the settlement calls for Liggett to phase in proposed Food and Drug Administration regulations regarding smoking by children and adolescents.

The regulations would prohibit the use of cartoon characters in tobacco advertising and limit the use of promotional materials and distribution of sample packages where minors are present.

However, Liggett would neither consent to FDA jurisdiction over the industry nor waive its objections to such jurisdiction, Brooke said in a news release.

Investor's Business Daily

RJR Ads Criticize Liggett Chief, Who’s Trying To Oust RJR Board

NEW YORK (AP) — RJR Nabisco Holdings Corp. struck back Friday at dissident shareholder Bennett LeBow, whose Liggett Group broke industry ranks this week in agreeing to settle lawsuits against major tobacco makers.

In a full-page advertisement, RJR said LeBow had wiped out $5 billion of stock market value for RJR and other tobacco-related companies in a single day with the settlement of a federal class-action lawsuit brought on behalf of smokers.

LeBow is the head of Brooke Group Ltd. which owns Liggett, the nation’s fifth-biggest tobacco company.

On Wednesday, LeBow agreed to settle the suit in Louisiana, in which smokers claimed they became addicted to the nicotine in cigarettes.

RJR owns the nation’s second-biggest tobacco company, R.J. Reynolds Tobacco. Along with other tobacco companies, it has vowed to continue defending itself in the Louisiana case.

But RJR has a special interest in what LeBow is doing because LeBow and his ally, financier Carl Icahn, together own a 5.8% stake in RJR.

The dissident shareholders are trying to unseat the RJR board next month because RJR has resisted their call for an immediate spinoff of its Nabisco food interests to RJR shareholders. Such a move would separate the food and tobacco businesses.

RJR has said it won’t consider a spinoff until 1997 or 1998, although it favors the idea of doing so.

In its ad, RJR said LeBow “demonstrated his reckless disregard for the long-term interests of RJR Nabisco shareholders” by agreeing to the settlement.

“We believe this irresponsible ploy was intended solely for the purpose of advancing LeBow’s and Carl Icahn’s proxy contest agenda,” it said.

A call to LeBow’s spokesmen for comment on the ads was not immediately returned. But LeBow has argued tobacco stocks could benefit in the long run if companies could put the threat of litigation behind them.

LeBow announced Friday that Liggett had agreed to settling lawsuits filed by five states who want tobacco companies to help pay the costs of treating tobacco-related illnesses that “a new economic model for the industry based on responsible coexistence instead of scorched earth confrontation can substantially increase stock valuations over time.”
