JOSEPH F. CULLMAN, 3RD
CHAIRMAN OF THE BOARD

May 20, 1971

Dear John:

I would like to comment further on the proposed City tax on the "tar" and nicotine content of cigarettes which I understand will soon come before the legislature for consideration. I have outlined to you in my letters of March 8 and March 24 the strong objections to this measure based both on the lack of scientific validity of its premise and the extreme difficulties it would pose from an administrative and marketing standpoint.

Mr. Malcolm L. Fleischer, Managing Director of the Retail Tobacco Dealers of America, has recently shown me your letter to him dated May 5 in which you defend the tax on two grounds: (1) it is not a tax on all cigarettes and therefore will not pose a threat to the City's and State's legitimate cigarette industry by reason of criminal activity; and (2) it is an attempt "to use the tax structure as an instrument in the attack on major threats to public health." Both grounds, it seems to me, are questionable.

The City's own figures indicate that the tax would apply to over 85% of the cigarettes marketed in New York. It seems quite certain that an additional 3-cent or 4-cent tax on 85% of all cigarettes sold in the City would not only aggravate the problem of bootlegging but also further encourage out-of-City purchases of the brands subject to the tax. Thus the City would not only fail to gain the anticipated revenue from this measure, but in the light of past experience, would also be likely to suffer a loss in revenue from the present 4 cents per pack excise tax.

The "public health" rationale is totally without scientific foundation. There is no evidence which establishes that "tar" and nicotine are significant in terms of human health or which justifies any distinction on health grounds between cigarettes with more or less than 17 milligrams of "tar" and 1.1 milligrams of nicotine.
