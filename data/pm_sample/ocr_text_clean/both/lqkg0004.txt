Questions to air on Jordan’s gambling

A few questions, while wondering whether it’s true Michael Jordan—run up $1.25 million in gambling debts while getting out-putted by San Diego businessman Richard Esquinas:

Considering Jordan’s 54-point explosion after being angered by media reports of his casino trip last week, would you bet against him hitting 60 Friday?

About the book title, Michael & Me: Our Gambling Addiction... my cry for help! Is it a cry or a well-timed move to cash in on a celebrity’s problems?

We keep hearing that gambling is a billion-dollar industry. Is Jordan chairman of the board?

Isn’t it time to go back and find out exactly why the Dream Team trained in Monte Carlo?

Every story so far about Jordan’s gambling has detailed mammoth losses. Don’t he ever win?

If Jordan’s gambling is so compulsive, it must have developed as an adult, otherwise, wouldn’t he have attended college at casino-convenient Nevada-Las Vegas?

SMOKE SCREEN: All this hoffer-thou fuss about the minor-league ban on uniformed personnel using tobacco products is just so much pompous garbage. It might be illegal for bush leaguers to use the devil wood, but their team owners still are free to indulge in tobacco advertising.

The game says it’s determined to eliminate any link between baseball and tobacco products, but took out in center field and chances are you’ll see some center fielder who’s just been forced to quit tobacco cold turkey standing near a billboard that glorifies the Martboro Man.

IT’S HARD to relate to Magic Johnson’s reason for not wanting the Los Angeles Clippers coaching job — that owner Donald T. Sterling wouldn’t sell him a chunk of the franchise.

Not even the greatest passer ever is entitled to make that kind of demand when his career coaching record is 0-0.

FOR ALL THE grief Candlestick Park has taken over the years, perhaps now that the Giants’ Matt Williams and Barry Bonds are atop the National League home run leaders, it at least will stop being maligned for being such a bad hitting park. Likewise, its counterpart across the Bay, the Oakland Coliseum, which Jose Canseco blamed for costing him about 10 a year.

Canseco has moved on to Texas, but he’s not to be found in the HR leaders column. Meanwhile, the Bay Area parks have been home to four home run champs in the last six seasons, while the supposed hitting paradises of Fenway Park and Atlanta-Fulton County Stadium have produced zero.

DOES ANYONE ELSE And it amazing that tennis has seemed so downright eager to take away Monica Seles’ No. 1 ranking? Seles has had her share of detractors ever since that still-unexplained absence from Wimbledon two years ago, but the game should cut her some kind of rankings break for a tour absence that was caused by a knife-wielding fan. Instead, now it’s tennis itself that’s stabbing her in the back.

NOW THAT HE has moved on to the Atlanta Hawks, Lenny Wilkens no doubt will get the 69 victories he needs to match Red Auerbach’s all-time record of 638 during the 1994-95 season. No offense to the astute Mr. Wilkens, but plans for that celebration should be tempered by the fact he also already has 749 career losses—a whopping 270 more than Auerbach.

WHEN DALE MURPHY won back-to-back MVP awards at the age of 27, he seemed like a lock for the Hall of Fame. But now that he has finished his career with a good but not Cooperstown-like 398 homers, his chances don’t look nearly so good, particularly when he figures to be up against Nolan Ryan, George Brett and Carlton Fisk in his first year on the ballot.

Which leads us to today’s trivia question: Who’s the only other player to win back-to-back MVPs who’s not in the Hall? If you guessed Roger Maris, give yourself a gold asterisk.
