Ms. Held:
I am writing in response to Sections 1921 to 1954 of the PHS Act, 42 U. S. C. 300x-21-
330x-64. My first request is that the period extending the public comment period be
extended for at least 60 days. An extension to the comment period would help to ensure
that a more thoughtful approach to preventing teen smoking might be substituted for the
one proposed. Presently I object to the proposal for the following reasons:
1. It is not appropriate to use youngsters in undercover "sting" operations. It
places them in danger, sends signals to them that is acceptable to break the law if done for
a "good" cause, and neither provides them with the information, role models , nor
guidance they need to counter peer pressure to smoke.
2. The regs require states to take on additional burdens of time and money
without additional dollars.
3. If compliance is not assured, drug and alcohol prevention funds will be
withheld -- at a time when these programs are beginning to show modest gains. Drinking
while driving is still a leading cause of death among teenagers, and the spread of HIV is a
growing treat to young people in their twenties who have contracted the virus when they
were teens. This is the wrong time to jeopardize these programs, and the wrong hostage
to hold responsible for illegal sale of tobacco.
Thank you for considering this points:
Sincerely,
Sally Sinclair, Elementary Supervisor
