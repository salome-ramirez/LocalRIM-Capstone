Yo Tengo R.O.D.E.R. is a free educational program designed to help students understand conflict, identify potentially exacerbating behaviors and develop positive resolution strategies. The program addresses four aspects of conflict resolution: the natural presence of conflict in our lives; the importance of understanding others' life situations and perspectives; the factors that can send conflict to out of control and some essential strategies to successful conflict resolution. In the first part of each activity, you will guide students in responding to the situations and conflicts presented, analyzing the behaviors and consequences of those involved, and recognizing additional options for resolution. In the second part of each activity, students will work in small groups to role play conflicts and gain experience with conflict resolution techniques.

This program is designed to be used with a supplement to the social studies, he encourages skills in problem-solving.

Program Objectives
This Yo Tengo R.O.D.E.R. program is designed to:
• Encourage students to accept conflict and develop a positive approach to negotiation.
• Simulate common conflicts that students face grousing up and offer them a range of responses leading to responsible, productive resolutions.
• Provide an open forum in which students can share conflicts from their lives, develop empathy for others' situations and perspectives and sharpen skills in defusing potentially volatile situations.
• Motivate students to resist the conflicts created by peer pressure and empower them to make responsible decisions in such areas as smoking and drinking.
• Use role-play scenarios to practice effective conflict resolution strategies and reinforce the positive results of constructive conflict management.

Program Components
1. Four activity masters to reproduce as individual and group worksheets.
2. This teacher's guide which contains:
• suggestions for presenting each activity.
• tips on how to conduct role-play exercises.
• ideas for extending each activity.
3. A full-color wall poster to display in the classroom as an integral part of the program.
4. A brochure to distribute to parents through your students.
5. A teacher response card that allows you to comment on the program and receive free educational programs in the future.

Implementing the Program
Activity Masters: Make enough copies for individual students to use as worksheets. The activities are arranged in a planned education sequence, with activity four culminating the program lessons.

Poster: Display the poster in a highly visible area of the classroom. Encourage students to refer to it as they work through the activities in the program and conflicts in their own lives.

Parent Brochure: Distribute the brochure for students to share with their parents. Encourage students to discuss the conflict resolution skills they are learning with family members.

Group Work: Organize students into groups of four or five. Students may remain in the same group throughout the program or change groups for each new activity. If students are not experienced working in small groups or doing role plays, you may want to appoint a student leader in each group to lead these exercises and review some common guidelines to review with the class:
• Discussions and role plays should focus on an honest, open and respectful exchange of ideas and exploration of options. All group members' comments should be thoughtfully accepted and responded to.
• Students' privacy must be honored, especially in discussions about potentially intimate conflicts with parents or peers.
• A clear structure should be established for each activity, requiring students to stay on task, to refrain from interrupting or making disparaging remarks, and to listen calmly to one another's comments. Activity One

Conflict in Our Lives
Objectives
• To help students define conflict, recognize its key elements and accept its natural place in human interactions.
• To demonstrate the use of questioning to understand conflicts and help students develop proactive responses toward conflict resolution.
