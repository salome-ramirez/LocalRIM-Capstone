Mr. Donald P. Carnody, Director, Division of Health Protection
Office of Policy Development and Planning
Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health
5600 Fishers Tone, Room 174-17
Rockville, Maryland 20057

Dear Mr. Carnody:

Doctor Richmond has requested the National Jogging Association to send ideas for program initiatives in several broad subject areas that might assist the Departmental Talk Force on Smoking in the development of a national strategy to reduce cigarette smoking in the nation. Our Executive Director, Carnody, is responding, and he is also asking to discuss the very early headline of September 1977.

First, I strongly recommend the promotion of the brief TV spots which warn about the dangers of smoking. These should be aimed at several different groups of smokers. For example, those of all ages should be created with an explanation of the fact that many adolescent smokers disguise their social and emotional vulnerability behind the bravado of smoking. That early warning keeps them smoking, according to Dr. Gardner, quarter of the University of Michigan. Veteran smokers often respond to scare tactics. The physician who said he stopped smoking when he saw the terrifying effects of ephysema in his patients, might successfully persuade some of the older smokers. The potential of TV, properly used, is tremendous.

I have more faith in the educational approach than in regulatory activities. Force is often counter-productive, unless carefully managed. The Legal Action Arm of the Anti Smoking Community, called Action on Smoking and Health, 2010 H St., N.W., Washington, D.C., 20006, is better able toiment on major legal or other obstacles than I am. The Interagency Council on Smoking and Health, Suite 1901, 410 Park Avenue South, New York, N.Y. 10116, is a first course of information about methods and effectiveness of educational efforts.

In writing educational material we should remember that cigarette smoking is usually listed as the first risk factor predisposing to coronary heart disease, and that bronchitis, coughing a lot, and cancer do not cover all the deleterious effects of tobacco.
