DRAFT TESTIMONY - 1/21/00
Good [morning] [afternoon].
My Name is Matt Paluszek, and I am Philip Morris' Regional Director of
State Government Affairs for New Hampshire.
I want to thank the Committee for giving me an opportunity here today to
speak on behalf of Philip Morris USA in opposition to HB 1577, which
would revise the New Hampshire indoor smoking act to completely ban
smoking in all restaurants.
I will talk about our objections to HB 1577 in a few minutes, as well as offer
some of our ideas for more reasonable solutions to address the issue of
smoking in restaurants and other hospitality venues.
But first I would like to raise a few points that I believe are essential to
understanding Philip Morris USA's positions on Environmental Tobacco
Smoke -- also known as ETS -- and public smoking restrictions.
First, Philip Morris understands and agrees that ETS can be unpleasant
and annoying to non-smokers. We acknowledge that people should be
able to avoid being around unwanted ETS, particularly in places where they
must go, such as public buildings, public transportation and many areas in
the workplace.
We acknowledge that government agencies and other authorities have
concluded ETS is a health risk. And we recognize and accept that many
people have health concerns regarding ETS as a result.
While we may not agree with some of the science used to support these
conclusions, we do believe that reasonable public smoking restrictions are
appropriate in many instances to help minimize unwanted ETS.
We also acknowledge that there are public places where smoking should
not be permitted at all, such as elevators, places where a specific fire
hazard already exists, or rooms primarily occupied by children, such as
schools and daycare facilities.

Good [morning] [afternoon].

My Name is Matt Paluszek, and I am Philip Morris' Regional Director of State Government Affairs for New Hampshire.

I want to thank the Committee for giving me an opportunity here today to speak on behalf of Philip Morris USA in opposition to HB 1577, which would revise the New Hampshire indoor smoking act to completely ban smoking in all restaurants.

I will talk about our objections to HB 1577 in a few minutes, as well as offer some of our ideas for more reasonable solutions to address the issue of smoking in restaurants and other hospitality venues.

But first I would like to raise a few points that I believe are essential to understanding Philip Morris USA’s positions on Environmental Tobacco Smoke -- also known as ETS -- and public smoking restrictions.

First, Philip Morris understands and agrees that ETS can be unpleasant and annoying to non-smokers. We acknowledge that people should be able to avoid being around unwanted ETS, particularly in places where they must go, such as public buildings, public transportation and many areas in the workplace.

We acknowledge that government agencies and other authorities have concluded ETS is a health risk. And we recognize and accept that many people have health concerns regarding ETS as a result.

While we may not agree with some of the science used to support these conclusions, we do believe that reasonable public smoking restrictions are appropriate in many instances to help minimize unwanted ETS.

We also acknowledge that there are public places where smoking should not be permitted at all, such as elevators, places where a specific fire hazard already exists, or rooms primarily occupied by children, such as schools and daycare facilities.
