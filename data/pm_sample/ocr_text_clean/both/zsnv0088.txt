Dear All,
From the various discussions with you all the following 5 functions of 'point persons' for our scientific issues have been
distilled. Let me share this with all of you as a basis for our work or further discussions of the process.
The point persons responsibilities are:
To 'drive' the review process from a nonsystematic collection of materials (stage 1) to a systematic collection of
materials (stage 2) to the scientific review (stage 3) and, if required, to a scientific position (stage 4). We agreed in HK
that for high-priority issues this process and its milestones will be spelled out by the point person in an operational
mini-plan.
To determine the expert team that works with him/her and the procedures under which the team moves forward.
To be the 'master of the issue folder, i.e. to determine the content of the file cabinet.
To coordinate the review process and establishment/maintenance of the file cabinet with the company experts, if
they are not part of from the WSA organization.
To identify with his/her team research gaps in the respective field (see my note dated Dec, 15, 1998) and
communicate this to the group and WSA management.
These responsibilities, like other issues related to the establishment of our scientific reviews, can be discussed at our
WSA conference call or worldwide meetings. Please let me know, if you have any questions or suggestions.
Best regards,

Subject: Scientific issues - point person responsibilities

Dear All,

From the various discussions with you all the following 5 functions of 'point persons' for our scientific issues have been distilled. Let me share this with all of you as a basis for our work or further discussions of the process.

The point persons responsibilities are:

• To 'drive' the review process from a nonsystematic collection of materials (stage 1) to a systematic collection of materials (stage 2) to the scientific review (stage 3) and, if required, to a scientific position (stage 4). We agreed in HK that for high-priority issues this process and its milestones will be spelled out by the point person in an operational mini-plan.

• To determine the expert team that works with him/her and the procedures under which the team moves forward.

• To be the 'master' of the issue folder, i.e. to determine the content of the file cabinet.

• To coordinate the review process and establishment/maintenance of the file cabinet with the company experts, if they are not part of from the WSA organization.

• To identify with his/her team research gaps in the respective field (see my note dated Dec. 15, 1998) and communicate this to the group and WSA management.

These responsibilities, like other issues related to the establishment of our scientific reviews, can be discussed at our WSA conference call or worldwide meetings. Please let me know, if you have any questions or suggestions.

Best regards,
Roger
