ASH OF CIGARETTE PAPER AT 525°C AND ACID INSOLUBLE ASH OF CIGARETTE PAPER

I. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY

The residue after ignition of cigarette paper at 525°C for 2½ hours is reported as the percent ash. The organic materials are combusted and the inorganic materials such as calcium, potassium, and magnesium salts, inorganic phosphates, sulfates, silica, carbonates, iron, and titanium remain as ash. Some of the inorganics such as potassium, sulfur and chlorine are lost by volatilization. The soluble portion of the ash is dissolved in 2N HCl, and the insoluble ash is determined gravimetrically on the residue.

This method is primarily used to estimate the carbonate content in paper which may affect the efficiency of laser perforation. Total Ash of Cigarette Paper (Method Number P-14) remains the routine method for determination of cigarette paper ash.

The relative standard deviation at the 2-Sigma level is ±2% for ash for a cigarette paper monitor selected to contain acid insoluble ash. This paper monitor yields 27% acid insoluble ash and the relative standard deviation at the 2-Sigma levels is ±3% for acid insoluble ash.

II. SAMPLE REQUIREMENTS

Total sample recommended: 4 grams
Sample recommended per determination: 1 gram
Replicate determinations normally made: 2
Man-hours per determination: 1 hour
Man-hours for replicates: 1.5 hours/2 determinations
Total elapsed time: 9 hours/2 determinations
