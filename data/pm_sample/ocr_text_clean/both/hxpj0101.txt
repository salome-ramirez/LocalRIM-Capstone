Anti-tobacco fight moves back to state level

By MICHELLE KOIDIN
Associated Press Writer

SAN DIEGO (AP) — With the anti-smoking bill dead in Congress, California and other states are stepping in with lawsuits targeting Big Tobacco for billions of dollars in health costs.

Plaintiffs in five major lawsuits consolidated by a San Diego judge want to go to trial in February. Meanwhile, state officials say California and several other states will continue negotiations for a nationwide multibillion dollar settlement.

Superior Court Judge Ronald S. Prager, who combined the five lawsuits on Monday, said he would recommend to the state Judicial Council that the case be tried in San Diego. The plaintiffs and the tobacco companies agreed on the consolidation.

In the combined lawsuit, the state of California, three cities and 15 counties are demanding reimbursement for tobacco-related costs imposed on state health programs such as Medi-Cal.

"We're on two tracks," explained Rob Stutzman, a spokesman for state Attorney General Dan Lungren. "One is full-speed ahead with our lawsuit. ... At the same time, as a separate effort, we are continuing to join our colleagues from other states at the table in exploring the potential for a settlement."

Lungren, the Republican nominee for governor, was campaigning in Irvine Tuesday. He said "active negotiations" continued but "we'll proceed to trial if we have to."

California got in line behind other states in their lawsuit quest. The first scheduled to go to court is Washington, in mid-September. Arizona, Oklahoma and Massachusetts are to follow.

Four states — Texas, Florida, Minnesota and Mississippi — have settled cases with the industry in the past year for a total of about $39 billion.
