ProjectTitle: Molecular Markers of Benzo[a]pyrene-Induced Atherosclerosis
Investigator(s): Alvaro Puga, Timothy Dalton, Marian Miller, Mario Medvedovic
J. Kevin Kerzee
Institution: Department of Environmental Health
University of Cincinnati College of Medicine
Abstract: In the space below, please provide a descriptive summary of your proposed research project.
The objective of this application is to study the molecular signature of ischemic heart disease
induced by exposure to benzo[alpyrene (B[a]P), a major carcinogenic agent in tobacco. Heart
disease is the major cause of mortality and morbidity in industrialized nations, affecting about 1.5 million
individuals in the USA annually. Many predisposing primary and secondary risk factors are known,
including elevated serum levels of cholesterol and triglycerides, high blood pressure, diabetes, obesity, and
genetic, psychosocial and behavioral factors. Cigarette smoking is also a risk factor in heart disease, but
little is known of the molecular mechanisms responsible for disease induction by tobacco components, due
in part to lack of suitable animal models and in part to social disinterest and stigmatization. Molecular
studies in this field are clearly needed, because they may help uncover early markers of disease incidence
vital in the identification of signaling pathways involved in disease development. This is of particular
importance in the study of heart disease induced by B[a]P, an abundant environmental pollutant resulting
from incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and a model compound for more than 400 other critical
environmental contaminants. B[a]P is a ligand for the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR), a ligand-activated
transcription factor responsible for the regulation of many detoxification genes. AHR-null mice, having
a homozygous deletion of the Ah receptor gene, are available in our laboratory. The objective of this
proposal is to use these mice and their AHR-positive counterparts to test the hypothesis that activation
of the Ah receptor by B[a]P causes a reprogramming of gene expression in the vascular
endothelium. As a result, endothelial cells release growth factors and signaling molecules that
induce proliferative changes in neighboring smooth muscle cells, leading to plaque formation. We
will test this hypothesis by measuring and analyzing global expression profiles of selected genes critical in
cell signaling, cell cycle regulation and apoptosis, combined with pathological and morphological analysis
of B[a]P-induced atherosclerosis. Results from these experiments will help delineate the role of
environmental risk factors in the development of ischemic heart disease.
