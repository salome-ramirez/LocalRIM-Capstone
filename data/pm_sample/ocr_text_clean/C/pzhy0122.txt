These announcements come on the heels of BUD's moves in the United Kingdom whereby BUD will be responsible for all
marketing. sales, and distribution of its products in the United Kingdom, just as it will be in Japan. On the European
Continent, BUD continues its negotiations with Budvar, the Government-owned brewer in Czechoslovakia. Budvar has the
rights to the Budweiser name on the European Continent, and, in fact, has its own Budweiser beer, a product quite different
from Anheuser's Budweiser (darker, etc.). We believe that BUD is interested in purchasing a minority-sized position in
Budvar. The agreement would involve BUD in the marketing of both Budweisers, possibly under some sort of royalty
arrangement with Budvar. It would not be at all surprising to see an agreement between Anbeuser-Busch and Budvar entered
into this year.
What all of this appears to indicate is that BUD's first priority after the domestic beer business is the international beer
business-more than snack foods, entertainment (Busch Gardens and Sea World), baked goods, or the metal container business.
This is not to say that the other lines of business will not remain important to the company, but rather that wben all is said and
done, the beer business outside of the U.S. is most likely to be the additional vehicle adding to the company's per share
earnings on a meaningful basis over the longer term.
On a separate, albeit related, note, we would point out that BUD's international moves are coming at the same time that Miller
is adopting a more aggressive posture towards the beer business outside of the U.S. Specifically, in recent months Philip
Morris 1 (MO, S64) has bought 20% of Molson Breweries in Canada and acquired a nearly 8% share of Femsa, Mexico's other
large brewer. It would not be at all surprising to see MO increase its stake in Femse at some point in the not too distant future.
Indeed, we wouid look for Miller to make further investments in the international beer business, concurrent with comperable
moves by BUD. In a nutshell, we have seen serious moves by both BUD and MO to become major forces in the internationst
beer business, and we view these moves as just the beginning of a series of investments by each company during the remaindes
of the 1990's.
