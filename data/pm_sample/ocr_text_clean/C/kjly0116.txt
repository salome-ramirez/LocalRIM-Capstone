Subject: TRIP REPORT -- CONFERENCE ON ORGANIC AND BIOLOGICAL CHEMISTRY OF
CARCINOGENIC AND CARCINOSTATIC AGENTS CONTAINING NITROGEN-NITROGEN
BONDS; HARPERS FERRY, WEST VIRGINIA, MAY 17-21, 1985
The above meeting was held at the Cliffside Inn just outside of Harpers
Ferry. Approximately 100 people were in attendance. Most of these are employed
in universities and research institutes, with a few from government, and very
few from industry. In effect, it was an international meeting, since there were
as many countries represented as for the Banff meeting on nitrosamines in 1983.
In fact, many of the principal people in nitrosamine research in the world were
there.
There were three full days of oral presentations, and one evening of poster
sessions. There were papers on various aspects of the chemistry, metabolism,
and biological activity of nitrosamines and a number of related classes of
compounds. The papers were all presented in single sessions. The overall
quality of the papers was excellent, with a few exceptions.
There were no abstracts or preprints available. Consequently, the only
information on most of the papers is the notes I took, and some of the speakers
covered the material very rapidly. This was due to the fact that they were only
expecting about 30 papers, and had planned to allow 20-25 minutes for each. The
large number of papers accepted forced the limitation of the presentations to 12
minutes. However, I was able to still get a lot of useful information which I
have summarized below in the order of presentation.
S. S. Hecht (AHF): Chemistry, DNA binding, and carcinogenesis of TSNA and
related model compounds. NDMA, NNN and NNK were bioassayed in F344 rats for
tumorigenicity. NNK was more potent than NDMA. The annual exposure of snuff
dippers to NNK is 0.3 mmol/Kg. The relative rate of methylation of DNA was
measured by HPLC/fluorescense. DMNA methylates DNA faster than does NNK. O-6
Methyl guanine was the compound measured. Obviously, other factors are
involved, since the results of the DNA methylation studies do not correlate with
the biological results. Pyridyl oxobutylation gives an intermediate which is
mutagenic. The adduct was isolated and identified -- a deoxyguanosine adduct.
A Deoxycytidine adduct was also isolated in 10% yield. NNN adducts with DNA
were also found -- there were 5'- and 2'- adducts. Keto nitrosourethanes are
potent mutagens, but aldehyde-nitrosourethanes are non-mutagenic.
