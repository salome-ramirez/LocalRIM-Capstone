Following two consecutive week-to-week declines, Marlboro's weekly share increased +0.2
points versus week ago to 24.5%. On a cumulative basis, Marlboro's share (7wk) was 24.5%,
+3.0 points above base.
Marlboro's average retail carton price (7wk cume.) was $16.36, -$2.80 below base. For the w/e
July 11, Marlboro's average price declined to $16.21 per carton, its lowest level since the
implementation of MLP.
Marlboro has realized the greatest share gain in Region 5 (+3.6 points) where the brand has also
experienced the greatest cumulative decline in price relative to Winston (-$3.07 vs. -$1.67). See
graphs on the following pages for a comparison of Marlboro and Winston by region.
Marlboro's cumulative share in IRI markets with a 50¢ off MLP promotion (excluding NY state)
has increased +4.3 points over base while its average carton price has declined -$3.67. See
graphs on the following pages for a comparison of Marlboro and Winston by MLP promotion
level.
Marlboro's MLP driven gain was partially offset by losses to other PM Premium and
Discount products of -0.5 and -0.7 points, respectively (cume. versus pre-MLP level),
resulting in a +1.8 point net-to-company contribution.
An estimation of the source of Marlboro's cumulative gains indicates that the brand is sourcing 20
approximately 55% of its growth from the Discount category (20% from Deep Discount).
Weekly Indus try volume continues to trend above its pre-MLP levels (see graph below), while
the percent of volume sold as cartons also increases (+1.6 points cume. versus base). This
suggests that MLP and competitive programs may be helping to reduce trade class shifting out of
supermarkets as carton purchases become more affordable.
