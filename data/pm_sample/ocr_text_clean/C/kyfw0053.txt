Subject: Proposal by SEiTA to establish a European Tobacco Research Institute
Dear Rick,
In September 99, Helmut and I attended a conference organized by the French cigarette manufacturer
SElTA. This was the third venue of the so-called Journées Scientifigues du Tabac. These conferences
gather representatives of European tobacco growers, processors and manufacturers.
This year's title of the conference was "Contribution of Research for Development of a 'clean' tobacco".
One day was devoted to scientific papers and the other day consisted of a workshop aimed at suggesting
"The studies and projects which should be undertaken in Europe to develop suitablé tobacco meetng the
present requirements of all the tobacco world".
The minutes of this workshop list all the proposals in a typical one-sheet format. They were distributed
earlier this month and I understand that Helmut sent you a copy of this document.
Among different topics suggested, one set of proposals for mid/long-term research is meant to address the
«Reduction of risks for the smoker». Helmut proposed that a European Tobacco Pesticide Committee be
set up.
Let me guote here the summary of the minutes, which relates to the main reason of my writing this note to
you.
«A great majority of the participants also admitted that the structure best suited to these different objectives
would be
– a European-centre for basic research which would take advantage of the grouping of technical and
human resources, and would deal with all expectations and suggestions coming from the tobacco "filière".
- a network organization for applied research along with technical supervision and training, relying
on regional or national experimental stations as well as the existing national centres.
The Institut de Bergerac, thanks to research/industry synergy from which it has benefited until now,
represents a research and development tool appreciated by all. Furthermore, SElTA is ready to modify the
organization and structure of its institute with view of opening a European partnership, and contacts already
taken with French growers are looking towards this possibility. The "clean" tobacco challenge can only
reinforce the validity of this opening»
As to the funding of such an institute, there is hope that part of the money could come from the EU
Commission. Inđeed, a fraction (2% if I am not mistaken) of the EU subsídies to tobacco growing is kept in
a fund to improve tobacco quality. This fund is currently underutilized and the idea is that part of the money
could be used to fund scientific research. An other part of the money could come from SElTA and other
members of the tobacco industry. All this is still vague for me at this stage.
Yesterday, I got an invitation from Bernard DUMERY, Head of Research at SEITA, to participate in an inter-
company technical committee. The latter should review and revise a proposal being established by SEiTA
to outline the structure of the contemplated European Institute and to provide cost and resource estimates
for the proposed projects.
They intend to work at a rather high pace with their first proposal being distributed within two weeks to the
committee, committee final proposal elaborated in December, presentation to the newly founded Tobacco
House early January and to the EU Commission at a later date to be defined.
I have discussed this with Allen and we both agree that the principle of establishing a European Research
Institute is a good one. It could do a good job to help the European growers improve their product quality,
which they need if they want to keep the EU subsidies on the long term. The institute could also do a good
job to address some of the controversial aspects of the products, e.g., the above-mentioned «Reduction of
risks for the smoker». These efforts should contribute to projecting a more realistic and positive image of
the industry as a whole.
