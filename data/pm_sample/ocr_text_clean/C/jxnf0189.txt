CIGARETTES AND HEALTH: A NEW LOOK AT THE EVIDENCE ARTICLE 4
NOBODY HAS YET PROVED SMOKING CAN BE BLAMED
FOR ANY DISEASE, TOBACCO INDUSTRY CONTENDS
In the first three articles in this series, Dr.
Jeese E. Edwards, president of the American
Heart Association, examined the "new fallacies"
that have developed over cigarette smoking and
health. This final article, by a tobacco industry
spokesman, disputes Dr. Edwards' arguments.
BY WILLIAM KLOEPFER JR.
Vice-President, The Tobacco Institute
DESPITE THE ASSUMPTIONS and statements of the first three
articles in this series, the role of tobacco in health is still very
much in dispute.
In the recent words of one authority, the evidence is "a
complex and hardly consistent picture showing a profusion of in-
consistencies and inversions." Even evidenee which some people orce
regarded as quite persuasive has had its claimed importance cast into
further doubt by more recent research.
The tobacco industry continues to be concerned about any
possible relationships between smoking and health. It has contributed
more than $20 million to independent scientists to study the questioa.
Thus far, however, nobody has been able to demonstrate any
substance, as found in cigarette smoke, that is responsible for any
human disease.
It is true that a number of studies have shown some statistical
association between smoking and a nuwber of diseases (some of them
very odd ones, such as accidents and backache). The existence of
an association, however, does not and cannot prove a cause-and-effect
relationship, as all competent statisticians agree.
For instance, the steady rise in cigarette smoking over the
years has been matched by an equally steady decline in stomach cancer.
Nobody, however, has claimed that smoking prevents stomach cancer.
