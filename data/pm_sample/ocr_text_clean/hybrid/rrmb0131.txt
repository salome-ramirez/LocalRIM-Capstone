Mr. J. E. Lincoln May 5, 1960
H. Wakeham
Comment on Preliminary and Final Reports from Consumer Tests of
Smoker Preferences
Please refer to reports from Ed Gray, such as the one of March 30
entitled "Preliminary Report Philip Morris with 126 Paper (Project
VI/IX)" and one dated April 14 entitled "Preliminary Report Low Tar
Winston vs. Marlboro."
You will recall that in these reports you say there seems to be a slight
preference one way or the other but that the results are not statistically
significant. I feel there is considerable likelihood management will
misinterpret this kind of a statement by concluding that the positive
preference has been established. I am sure that in many cases the
reader has no appreciation of the very real hazard of drawing an
incorrect conclusion with the limited returns.
I have termed this a form of "statistical hedging. " In the preliminary
report you implied that a preference may exist, but you hedge your
statement against the possibility of being wrong by saying that the
differences are "not statistically significant. "
I have put to Tom Budne the question as to how we can express results
of this type without giving the reader a false impression of their validity.
Tom's reply is attached for your study.
My own feeling is that something like the following might be said; for
example, in the report from Ed Gray dated March 30, referred to above,
"With about half of the questionnaires returned, the results are incon-
clusive. If the data so far obtained with only 171 testers are supported
in the final results, the 126 paper Philip Morris may be slightly preferred
(by the slim ratio of 53/47) to the current regular 70 mm Philip Morris
against which it was tested. In drawing this conclusion on the limited
returns, however, we run the risk of being wrong 44% of the time. We
can be certain 95% of the time that the preference for 126 paper is not
greater than the 59/4l ratio. At the same time, there is a 22% chance
that more than half of the total population may prefer the regular Philip
Morris without the 126 paper."
I will be very happy to discuss this problem further with you if you wish.
I know that our management people are anxious to have these preliminary
results, but feel that we should give them some realistic idea as to the
risk being taken in basing our conclusion on these preliminary data.
