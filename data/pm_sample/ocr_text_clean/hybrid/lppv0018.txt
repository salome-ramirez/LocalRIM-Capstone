Subject: . Senator Cranston.
With respect to Senator Cranston's willingness to become more
"open minded" about the question of self-extinguishing cigarettes,
you requested that I ascertain whether any real purpose would be
served by us meeting with Cranston on this subject. Following a
personal visit to Richmond for discussions with Bob Seligman, Leo
Meyer and Andy Kallianos, a report on a meeting by the latter two
gentlemen with Mr. John Krasny of the National Bureau of Standards
who has been conducting the Bureau's tests on this matter, and a
meeting with Roy Greenaway, the long time top Senate aide to
Cranston, I have concluded that meeting with Cranston would not.
be worthwhile.
In meeting with Greenaway, I put the following question. to him,
"What does Senator Cranston's expression of greater open-
mindedness mean in legislative terms?" The response, not in
exact order, was as follows:
1. The Senator is always open minded.
2. He wants this problem resolved by the companies and
is willing to listen to any alternative solution we might
develop.
3. "I can't put words in the Senator's mouth" but. think
that Cranston would be willing to accept the trade-off
between the rise in tar, nicotine and Co levels in return
for faster extinguishing cigarettes because he believes that
the former affects individuals who have chosen to harm
themselves, whereas the latter affects the lives of others.
4. Cranston is not pushing for the bill.
5. It is useful to continue to say things about the
problem in order to keep attention focused on it in the
same manner that Cranston has found it useful in the past
to introduce again and again a certain doomed environmental
bill in order to bring attention to the possibility of
extinction of a. Nevada endangered species by over-development
and thereby thwart the developers through the adverse
publicity.
