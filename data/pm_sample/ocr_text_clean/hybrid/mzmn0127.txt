DAILY NEWS Friday, November 28, 1986
Burned up over smoking
The New York State Public Health Council is in over
its head. It has just given preliminary okay to a set of rules
on smoking that are among the harshest in the nation.
Lighting up wouid be verboten in most public areas. Cars,
private homes and large pastures would be among the few
places left in the state where smoking is permitted.
Health Commissioner David Axelrod says the coun-
cil's rules are justified "in' the communal interest." A
powerful point. Smoking is unhealthy, both for the puffer
and those who breathe what he or she exhales.
But good intentions don't justify the council's grab-
bing for power beyond its reach. Only the Legislature,
made up of elected representatives of the people, has the
authority to enact such sweeping changes in the law.
The courts almost certainly will overturn the rules on
precisely this point—the decision must be made by legisla-
tors. What's the use of the council's marching up the
no-smoking hill only to be knocked back down again?
That's setting back the very cause it professes to support.
