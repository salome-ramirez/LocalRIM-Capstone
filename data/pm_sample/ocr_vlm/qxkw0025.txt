Younger Andretti
Takes Marlboro 500

BROOKLYN, Mich. (AP) — Michael Andretti took advantage of his father's continuing hard luck to win the Marlboro 500 Sunday at Michigan International Speedway, the fastest 500-mile race in Indy-car history.

The younger Andretti inherited the lead when Mario Andretti fell out of the race with an engine failure after building a two-lap lead over his son just past the halway point of the 250-mile event.

The victory from the pole was the younger Andretti equalled the feat of his father here in 1948 and made Michael the seventh different winner in as many 500-mile races on Michigan's fast 2-mile oval.

Michael, 24, easily held off Indianapolis 500 winner Al Ursner and Bobby Rahal over the last 94 lap to pick up his second victory of the season, the fifth of his blossoming Indy-car career and his first triumph over 500 miles.

The winner, who still trails CART-PPG series driving leader, and finishing champion Rahal 119 points to 118, averaged 171.400 mph in a race slowed by only two caution periods.

That was faster than the 170.722 run by Rahal in winning the 1966 Indy 500, the fastest previous Indy-car race.
