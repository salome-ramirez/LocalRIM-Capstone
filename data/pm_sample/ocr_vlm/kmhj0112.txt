The influence of population trends on cigarette consumption - your request of December 9

The changes in the age composition of the population of the United States over the next ten years bode well for the tobacco industry. The most dramatic change in the next five years will be the 27.8% increase in the number of 21 to 24 year olds. This is over four times the increase expected in total population. Only slightly less dramatic is the 22.1% increase between 1966 and 1971 in the number of persons 25 to 29 years old. During the 1971-76 period the 21-24 age group will increase at about the same rate as total population, but the age group 25-29 will increase 27.6%, and ages 30-34 will increase 21.7% (Table 1 and Chart 1). The principal cause of these increases, of course, is the post-war baby boom. One indication of the magnitude of the effect of the baby boom is the number of people reaching a given age in two consecutive years: Between July 1, 1965 and June 30, 1966 about 2,777,000 people reached 20 years of age, but between July 1, 1966 and June 30, 1967 about 3,740,000 people will reach age 20, an increase of nearly 35% over the preceding year. In the next few years the number of people reaching age 20 will drop slightly and then increase slowly, reaching four million by 1976.

These changes in the age composition of the population are of considerable significance for all of our consumer products, for they mean that each year there will be about 30% more potential new smokers (and shavers) than during the 1950's.

The effect of these population changes should result in an average annual increase in cigarette consumption of 1.50% between 1966 and 1971 and 1.65% between 1971 and 1976, in contrast to the 1.31% increase attributable to population change between 1961 and 1966. Similarly, the increase in the number of smokers attributable to the changing population should average 1.66% per year from 1966 to 1971 and 1.73% per year from 1971 to 1976, in contrast to the average annual increase of 1.53% from 1961 to 1966.
