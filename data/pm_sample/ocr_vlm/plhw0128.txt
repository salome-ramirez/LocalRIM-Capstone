Memorandum to: A. Holtzman
From: Dr. Israel Rappaport
Re: Continuation -- World Conference on Smoking & Health
New York, September 11-13, 1967

Excerpts from (2nd part) Chapter III, etc. of interesting remarks included in discussions.

Chapter III - Page 97

Sir George Godber (British Ministry of Health) under title of the British and Norweigen Experiences: This is "an exercise which is quite different from almost anything else that we who are concerned with preventive medicine have tried or now try to do."

"We know that chronic bronchitis with its large toll of mortality and even larger morbidity is substantially attributable to smoking."

P.103 "We may be dealing with a situation in which a climate of opinion will be gradually formed by the steady pressure of information about the hazards until the smoking of cigarettes becomes no longer an acceptable activity in public." P.105 "There is much we do not know about the pharmacology of nicotine and its effect on the maintenance of the disease." P.107 "There is evidently still a sufficient proportion of the public that believes that there are differences of opinion among the experts."

Perhaps the disability and deaths from chronic bronchitis which are so largely the result of cigarette smoking and are in sum no less than the risk of lung cancer, as well as the greater risk of early heart disease are too little emphasized.

P.108 "Karl Ewang Norway -- Compared to other countries Norway demonstrates a relatively favorable position with respect to death rates from diseases causally connected with cigarette smoking." At present 2/3 of males and 1/4 of adult females smoke.

P.112 "Referring to Mavvison, etc the Committee facts that are must accept the possibility of negative effect of information campaigns changing peoples level of information but not their behavior, the tension will increase which may in fact lead to nerviously conditioned increase in consumption of tobacco."

Chapter IV - Dr. Wm. H. Stewart, Public Health Service

p.120 "People are deeply interested...want to know how scientific knowledge can be put to work."

It has been demonstrated that increased dosage of tars and nicotine produced increased hazards. On this point epidemiology and laboratory science are in impressive agreement."
