Still waiting
On March 31 'The Globe and Mail' published Spider Robinson's column Taking The Air in which he wrote that ever since the commercial airlines have insisted that flights be smoaked they have reduced inbound air circulation by 50 per cent. They have thereby issued a medium-sized ferns in jet fuel. As a result, passengers and crew spend every journey in a poorly ventilated Petri dish of shared viruses. Now I didn't know that, and I can only guess that very few other people did either. All week I scanned the paper looking for rare letters from airline passengers, denials from the airline industry and some investigative reporting by 'The Globe and Mail'. So far there has been nothing.

Am I the only person who read the column? Is The Globe and Mail so infatuated with regulation that it refuses to print any discussion about a subject with dire consequences for everyone who travels in airplanes? Did none of your editorial writers care to drive into the story? And what about the smokers' rights job? They should be jumping for joy. Turns out we were healthier when people were allowed to smoke in airplanes. Bring back the airlines and out with the bacteria.

Claire Mowat, Port Hope, Ont.
