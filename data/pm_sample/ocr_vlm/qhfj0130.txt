Dear Mr. Repussard,

Thank you for your letter of 30 November 1982 and the enclosed consultation document "Environmental standardization by CEN - A proposal for a general outline of activities".

After examination of this document, I can inform you that the Directorate-General for Transport can in principle support the recommendations put forward by CEN. The policy line appears to be fairly broad and unlikely to be incompatible with our own line of policy. We also endorse the idea that there should be an Environmental Working Group for the purpose of coordinating all CEN environmental standardization.

With regard to some specific issues, we would like to make the following comments.

The document does not appear to make reference to the standardization work carried out by the International Maritime Organization (IMO). Shipping is a global activity and therefore is best served by a global approach, particularly on issues already subject to regional rules. IMO is not only involved in the setting of standards for marine pollution prevention and monitoring equipment but also in the setting of rules for waste treatment.

We feel it would also be useful to make reference to the work of the GESAMP group, which is used by several UN agencies to assess criteria for dangerous and polluting goods. Alignment of standardization in this area is essential particularly for the purposes of multi-modal transport.

We can also endorse the idea of setting up a Task Force for Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA). The EIA is indeed evolving into a management tool, which contributes to the decision-making process. In this respect it is important to note that a lack of standardization in the evaluation techniques could lead to divergent results. However, we feel that close cooperation is essential on this matter, particularly with the Directorate-General for the Environment.

Mr. J. REPUSSARD
