Differential Distribution of the Mixed-Function Oxidase Activities in Rabbit Kidney

TERRY V. ZENSER, MICHAEL B. MATTAMMAL AND BERNARD B. DAVIS
Geriatric Research, Education and Clinical Center, VA Hospital, and Departments of Biochemistry and Medicine, St. Louis University, St. Louis, Missouri
Accepted for publication August 14, 1978

Abstract
Zenser, Terry V., Michael B. Mattammal and Bernard B. Davis: Differential distribution of the mixed-function oxidase activities in rabbit kidney. J. Pharmacol. Exp. Ther. 207: 719–725, 1978.

Microsomal mixed-function oxidase activities in renal cortex, outer medulla and inner medulla were characterized and compared to liver in control and 3-methylcholanthrene-treated adult rabbits. Renal cytochrome P-450 was more sensitive to induction by 3-methylcholanthrene than liver with a 5-fold increase in cortical content observed. Outer medullary cytochrome P-450 content was only demonstrated after 3-methylcholanthrene treatment, but inner medullary cytochrome P-450 content was not observed by spectral analysis. In control rabbits, renal aryl hydrocarbon hydroxylase and dimethylaniline demethylase activities were not measurable. Treatment with 3-methylcholanthrene resulted in a appearance of cortical and outer medullary aryl hydrocarbon hydroxylase but not of dimethylaniline demethylase activities. Aminopyrine demethylase activity was increased by 3-methylcholanthrene in renal cortex and outer medulla but not in liver. Lauric acid hydroxylase was the only specific mixed-function oxidase activity observed in the inner medulla. The renal cortex contained the highest lauric acid hydroxylase activity with liver and inner medulla exhibiting similar activity and the outer medulla containing the least activity. Lauric acid hydroxylase activity was inhibited by carbon monoxide and metyrapone in each area of the kidney. By contrast, α-naphthoflavone, inhibited cortical and outer medullary but not inner medullary lauric acid hydroxylase activity in 3-methylcholanthrene-treated rabbits. These results demonstrate the presence of specific mixed-function oxidases in each area of the kidney. The different sensitivity of renal and hepatic oxidases to 3-methylcholanthrene induction suggests that the genetic expression of these enzymes in kidney and liver may be different.

Cytochrome P-450's are unique hemoproteins which function as the terminal oxidase for the mixed-function oxidase system. This system is responsible for the oxidative biotransformation of many endogenous and exogenous compounds including carcinogens, fatty acids and many reactive agents. The mixed-function oxidase system has been extensively studied in liver and, to a lesser extent, in extrahepatic tissues. Comparative studies between hepatic and extrahepatic tissues have suggested the existence of multiple forms of cytochrome P-450 that differ from organ to organ and species to species (Atlas et al., 1977; Kawalek et al., 1975; Thomas et al., 1976). In addition, the genetic expression of mixed-function oxidase system enzymes differs between organs of the same species (Atlas et al., 1975, 1977; Hook et al., 1975; Lake et al., 1973).
