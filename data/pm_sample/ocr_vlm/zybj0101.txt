Marleau rejects declaring tobacco hazardous product

DENNIS WEEKERT
CANADIAN PRESS

OTTAWA – Health Minister Diane Marleau has rejected a proposal by anti-smoking activists to declare tobacco a hazardous product. It would not be appropriate to place tobacco under the Hazardous Products Act, Marleau said yesterday outside the Commons. The act is used to regulate everything from stuart toys to crisps. She would not say what measures she plans to replace the Tobacco Products Control Act which was gutted by the Supreme Court of Canada in September.

The act banned tobacco advertising and was deemed to violate the free speech guarantee in the Charter of Rights and Freedoms. "We've been working very hard and we will move as fast as we can and we will go as far as we can," Marleau said outside the Commons. She hopes to announce her intentions by the end of the year.

About 30 doctors and medical students marched on Parliament Hill yesterday to demand new tobacco control measures.

Many carried boxes containing pink buns and cigarette packs to underline that stuffed 109s are regulated under the Hazardous Products Act, but tobacco is not.

"It's been two months and we're anxious to hear from the government about what they're going to do to deal with the complete absence of effective tobacco control legislation," said Dave Williams of the Canadian Medical Association.

He said he is baffled as to why authorities are not regulated under the act, which he sees as strong enough to withstand a new court challenge. "It's an opportunity that's ready to be used."

The legislation permits the government to regulate the marketing, merchandising and manufacture of any products deemed hazardous.

Marleau said the act is intended to remove the hazards associated with a product, but there is no way that the hazards of smoking can be eliminated.

She said discussions with the tobacco industry are under way. Major tobacco companies have maintained to respect the advertising by while talks continue.

"I would imagine they (the industry) won't necessarily be pleased with some of the things I'm brining forward," said Marleau. "But even the tobacco industry is anxious to know what kind of rules will be there."

Marleau says she's lobbying in cabinet to have tobacco taxes increased.
