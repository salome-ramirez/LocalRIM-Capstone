15 April 1991

To the Members of the International HO-RE-CA:

STAYING AHEAD

An Initiative Grows ... Following our "issues alert" in the Ho.Re.Ca News (July 1989) concerning restaurant smoking restrictions and a special report on indoor air quality (October 1989), we now provide further information and suggestions on measures to stay in front of the issue.

If smoking is not yet a concern in your restaurants, this material can be used simply as background information. If, however, customers have begun to request separate seating for nonsmoking or government regulations are being considered, this program will help owners and operators to assess and adjust positively to their customers' smoking preferences. The materials in this kit suggest steps to assist national associations in developing campaigns which reflect their own individual and geographic perspective.

TAKING THE INITIATIVE

For the restaurateurs, this initiative grows from the conviction that creating a comfortable, congenial dining experience is a valued tradition and an individual art.

To preserve that tradition and counter prospects of unworkable regulations imposed from outside, the campaign takes the initiative to welcome smokers and nonsmokers and accommodate their preferences with equal regard. Also, it asks the customer to reaffirm the traditional courtesy that has permitted smokers and nonsmokers to get along harmoniously for generations.

WATCHDOGS AND EMPTY TABLES

For employees, the campaign provides a solution to serious problems that legislated smoking restrictions bring: empty tables; angry or ill-at-care customers; loss of jobs and revenues from lost business; the need for employees to act as policemen, referees or peacemakers in conflicts among customers.

ACCOMMODATION AND SATISFACTION

For customers, the program represents increased personal choice as well as a cooperative atmosphere conducive to a more pleasant, satisfying dining experience. We believe the campaign will help customers understand how mutual courtesy and respect can resolve conflict better than legal mandates. And they are likely to appreciate how eager the restaurateur is to accommodate their individual preferences.
