April 29, 1986
R&D Program Planning Meeting April 8-9, 1986 -- Follow-up
Bonded Ends
During and following the presentation of the Bonded Cigarette Ends status and
challenges, discussion primarily concerned three areas:
Heating of the binder solution - This was suggested as a means to increase the
electrical conductivity of the material. Conductivity sufficient to achieve
adequate charging of the particles has been achieved so far only by the addition
of salt. Heating of the binder will be considered as an additional or alter-
native means of achieving this result. It was pointed out that this would be
consistent with the use of heat to obtain microbiological stability and to lower
the viscosity of the binder and perhaps assist in drop formation.
Use of multiple jet nozzles - The largest commercial nozzles available are
capable of application rates only one-tenth to one one-hundredth of the rate
expected to be needed at production speeds. Nozzles capable of producing drops
large enough to achieve the desired application rates have been designed and
built in-house, but charging and deflection of the large drops with their lower
surface-to-volume ratio has not been demonstrated. If we are unable to achieve
the desired results with single large nozzles, a straight-forward scale-up if it
works, whether to launch into the development of a multiple nozzle system will
be considered. Mechanical and electronic design would become much more complex
than working with a single nozzle.
Computer contro] capacity - Concern was expressed regarding the computer
capacity required to control the fluid jet drops at production speeds. We
believe this potential problem area has been addressed by eliminating much of
the flexibility that would be required in a conventional printing application
and by hard wiring. It is not anticipated that computer capacity will be a
problem in this system.
