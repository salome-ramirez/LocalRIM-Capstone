draft osha talking points/gen. business
*
hard
I should be allowed the flexibility to tailor my
smoking policy to the preferences of my and employees.
My business already has a smoking policy that works.
don't need some bureaucrat in Washington telling us how
to run our establishment.
A "one-size-fits-all" rule imposed by a federal agency
won't work for all businesses. You can not treat my
company as if it were a Fortune 500 company or a small
neighborhood diner as a large restaurant.
OSHA should reconsider its proposal. It should adopt a
rule that provides flexibility so that business owners
can accommodate non-smokers and smokers.
OSHA's proposed rule would be especially burdensome for
a small business like ours. I can't afford to install
the separate smoking rooms that would be required under
the proposed regulation. I'm worried about losing both
customers and employees to larger businesses that can
provide such areas.
If these regulations are imposed as written they could
put a lot of companies out of business and a lot of
working Americans out of a job.
These regulations are too far reaching and
unnecessary. Most businesses in the U.S. already have
smoking policy in place. Launching yet another big,
federal government program to do what I am already
doing--better and more efficiently-- is a waste of time
and taxpayers' money.
Business owners will be responsible for any violations
of OSHA's workplace smoking policy. So, in addition to
the day-to-day responsibilities associated with running
my business, including complying with dozens of state,
federal and local regulations, already on the books, I
now get to wear a new hat -- "Smoke Police".
