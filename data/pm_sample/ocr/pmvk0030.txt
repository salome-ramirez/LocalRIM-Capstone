Small business looks for change in labor board
Karan Kerrigan
Arbitrary labor Jaws that are burdensome and unfair to smali businesses may
get an overhaul if legislation aimed to bring faimess to National Labor
Relations Board proceedings make it to President Clinton's desk.
‘The Congress is considering a small business labor relations board relief
package of bills which would address serious problems stemming from the
National Labor Relations Act and the procedures used by the labor board to
enforce these laws.
Small businesses are especially vulnerable when they are hit with frivolous
charges of unfair labor practices. These often arise when « business becomes
the target of a union organizing drive where hardball tactics are used to
financially squeeze the small employer into bowing to union demands.
The labor relations board may become the union accomplice in such cases if it
discards its impartiality in such disputes. Many claim the board has abandoned
its standing as neutral arbiter in Inbor relations, and legislation is desperately
needed to restore balance and fairness to its proceedings
"Impartiality and evenhandedngss shouldbe the NLRD's guiding principles.
‘The current board has a decidedly partisan stance as demonstrated by its many
unusual decisions and directives," said David Thompson, a workplace attomey
in Washington, D.C.
Four bitls moving through the Congress has gamered widespread support from
those who agree that the labor relations board has allowed its procedures to be
abused by unions to inflict economic harm on small businesses and their
employees.
"This package will level the playing field for small businesses as they face an
aggressive NLRB with vast expertise and resources," said Mr. Thompson.
The small business package includes:
‘The Truth in Employment Act
This legislation would remedy the unserupulous practice of "salting,"
According to the Independent Electrical Contractors, "salts" can best be
described as union organizers who seck employment with open shop employers
to harass or disrupt the business, The goal of salting isto drive open shops out
of existence through economic hardship caused by expensive labor relations
board proceedings.
‘The legislative measure would amend the National Labor Relations Act to
make clear tht an employer isnot required to hire any person wo seeks job
to promote interests unrelated to those of the employer. The bill would not
infinge upon cemployce's existing protections under the act, including the right
to organize.
Fair Access to Indemnity and Reimbursement
Small-business owners often cannot afford the qualified Jegal representation
to defend themselves against labor relations board charges. This act
amends the National Labor Relations Act to reimburse a small business or labor
organization prevailing against a labor board action the attomey's fees and
expenses used to defend itself,
‘The act applies to any employer with 100 employees or less, and a net worth of
no more $1.4 million.
“Filing and pursuing a NLRB charge is essentiaily costes to the charging
Party and extremely expensive forte respondent” said Peter Rousos, director
of corporate human relations for Gaylord
Tenn.
'ntertainment Corp. in Nashville,
‘Mr. Rouses testified on behalf of the U.S. Chamber of Commerce before the
‘House Committee of Education and the Workforce earlier this month.
‘According to Mr. Rousos, the bill would require labor board officials to be
more rigorous in their investigations, “especially when it may be readily
apparent that the charging party's true motive is merely coercion or harassinent
of the respondent."
Fair Hearing Act
Labor relations board chairman William Gould has been an outspoken
proponent of the "single-site" organizing rule—a proposal that would provide
unions an advantage tn organizing the multiunit locations of small businesses.
Mr. Gould's troubling proposal (in that he is supposed to be an arbiter of the
law not an advocate of causes) would overturn a fundamental of the
National Labor Relations Act~that the labor relations board's decisions are
based on the aspects of each situation, or each sepatate tnt,
To date, Congress has restricted board spending to prohibit Mr. Gould from
implementing his new rule, but many believe a legislative fix is in order to
codify the law. The legislation would stop the activist labor relations board
from proceeding with the rule.
Justice on Time Act
‘This legislation would shorten the often long delays in the processing of cases
bby the labor relations board. The board would be required to issue a
decision within one year on all unfair labor practice complaints in cases where
it is alleged that an employer has discharged an employee in an attempt to
encourage or discourage union membership.
The median time for Inbor relations board processing of all unfair labor practice
cases is 546 days. The goal of the bill isto ensure that employees who are
entitled to reinstatement will quickly get their jobs back, and employers will
not be saddled with large liability payments for backpay awards,
Although the business community supports the concept of this bill, there is
concer that complex cases do require longer deliberations.
‘These measures to protect small businesses will likely pass the Congress, but it
remains questionable as to President Clinton's support for the reforms,
Karen Kerrigan is president of the Smal! Business Survival Committee in
