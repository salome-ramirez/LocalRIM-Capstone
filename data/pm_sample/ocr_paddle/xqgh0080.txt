RESPONSES TO QUESTIONS POSED BY SENATOR GRASSLEY
(October 1997)
1. Who negotiated this settlement? Who was in the room? What interests
were represented?
The Proposed Resolution resulted from discussions among
representatives of state attorneys general, representatives of the public
health community, counsel to the plaintiffs in various class action lawsuits
and representatives of the five largest U.S. tobacco companies.
Accordingly, a range of groups involved in tobacco issues, including
government, smokers, and the public health community, were represented
in the negotiations with the industry's representatives. As a result, the
Proposed Resolution reflects a carefully balanced consensus among a
wide range of interests in public policy related to tobacco.
2. Title IV(D) of the settlement document states that "[ajli payments pursuant
to this Agreement...shall be deemed ordinary and necessary business
expenses for the year of payment". Further, you again stated this on page
10 of your written testimony submitted to the Senate Judiciary Committee.
I assume that this means that tobacco companies can deduct the total
amount of their industry payments when calculating their federal income
tax liability. Is this true? Do the tobacco companies, especially Philip
Morris, plan to deduct the full amount of the annual industry payments?
Our understanding of current tax law is that payments in settlement of the
monetary claims against any defendant, including claims for punitive
damages, are deductible business expenses. The text in Title IV(D), to
which this question refers, is intended to confirm such tax treatment,
including as to the "look back" surcharges. Philip Morris would expect to
deduct such annual industry payments.
3. Does the tobacco industry plan to pass through to consumers the entire
cost of the annual industry payments? During last week's Judiciary
