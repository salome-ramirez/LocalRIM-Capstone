Reduced Sidestream
Trim
Considerable attention has been devoted to the single-wrap Superslims. A rapid
analysis of QA data on cigarettes produced at Louisville through December would tend to
suggest that a consistent sidestream reduction of 70% would not be achieved with currently
specified 10-062A paper (53 g/m² basis weight, 10.5% MKP, and 10.5 Coresta), although
additional work must be done to confirm this supposition. It appears that the sidestream
reduction range which would be achieved using this paper would be 66-69%. As a
consequence, four approaches have been proposed to redesign the paper with the goal of
completing the redesign by early March.
The first approach is to modify the process which is used to coat and dry the paper to
increase the extent of the reaction between MKP and the calcium carbonate filler. As has been
reported previously, when this reaction is allowed to proceed to a greater extent, through either
increasing the temperature of the coating solution or increasing the drying time, the amount of
water-extractable phosphate decreases, and the sidestream reduction increases. The four
papers, reported on previously, made using the in-line size press in Spotswood (8.5, 10.5, and
12.5 Coresta, with the 10.5 Coresta produced at two different drying regimens) were received
last week and have been submitted for analysis. However, a rapid analysis carried out at
Spotswood of the two 10.5 Coresta papers indicates that there may not be significantly less
extractable phosphate in the paper dried for a longer time. Nevertheless, cigarettes will be
made at Louisville next week. It is possible that sufficient control of the process can not be 2020020057
obtained with the in-line size press at Spotswood, which would preclude this approach.
(Although, it is likely that this approach would succeed if the coating and drying were carried
out at Ancram, the additional expense makes this option unacceptable.)
