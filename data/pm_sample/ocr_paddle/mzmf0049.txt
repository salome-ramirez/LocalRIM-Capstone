"International News: Finland--Major Restrictions Passed on
Public Smoking"
Tobacco International (10/94) Vol. 196, No. 14, P. 7
Finland's environmental ministry officials told the Finnish parliament
the country's new anti-smoking legislation will become law in March
1995. It will ban smoking in all public places, including movie 2058442225A
theaters, night clubs, and concert halls, but it will permit smoking in
restaurants and hotels. A separate law currently working its way
through parliament proposes restricting restaurant and hotel smoking
to well-ventilated areas by the end of 1995. Companies will be
required to set aside specially ventilated rooms, since smoking will be
prohibited in all offices that contain more than one employee. The
age limit for purchasing tobacco will be raised from 16 to 18 years.
and tobacco marketers will be banned from using cigarette brands to
sell other products, such as Camel boots and Marlboro t-shirts.
"Tabacalera Studying Opportunities Abroad"
Reuters (11/14/94)
Spanish tobacco group Tabacalera SA says it is actively looking for
overseas markets to identify expansion opportunities. Cigarette
consumption in Spain has fallen 0.7 or 1.0 percent a year, says
Financial Director Calixto Rios, adding that Eastern Europe, Latin
America, and Asia have potential for growth. Tabacalera's group
nine-month profit rose to 13.05 billion pesetas from 6.40 billíon during
the same period last year, due to higher sales of premium brands, a
crackdown on smuggling, and lower costs as a result of layoffs.
Sales of Philip Morris' Marlboro and R.J. Reynolds' Winston, which
Tabacalera makes under license, were particularly high. Rios says
Tabacalera still expects full-year pre-tax profits to be around 30 to 35
percent above last year's, which would give a parent company result
of 14 to 14.5 billion pesetas, compared with 10.7 billion in 1993.
Contraband cigarette sales dropped sharply this year, but next year's
increases in value-added and excise taxes will add 10 percent to the
price of cigarette packs and could inspire smugglers to new efforts,
