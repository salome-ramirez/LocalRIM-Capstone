TCRO
TO Dr. T. S. Osdene DATE: November 11, 1966
FROM R. D. Carpenter
SUBJECT: Tobacco Chemists' Conference Observations
The following comments are based on discussions with several
people at the Tobacco Chemists' Conference
R.J.Reynolds Tobacco Company
I toured the Reynolds' research laboratories with Dr. James Dobbins
Of particular interest is the fact that Reynolds is investigating
high boiling constituents of TPM by gas chromatography. The
comment was made that high filtration cigarettes were "worse"
than nonfilter cigarettes. I do not know the identity of the
constituents which were referred to,but I infer them to be
polynuclear hydrocarbons. The comment was made that nonfilters
are better than Winston, which in turn is better than True.
When Winston and True filters were investigated,neither cigarette
was improved but rather the reverse occurred. This work was being
done in the organic research group by a man who has only an
analytical background. I wondered if this group is working to
alter the polynuclear hydrocarbon content by the use of additives
or filters
Reynolds has five or six laboratories for radiochemistry and an
additional two rooms for counting. They have a 20 x 35 greenhouse
which is apparently being used for growing tagged tobacco and
perhaps other plants
It was stated also that they recently obtained a BET apparatus
Reynolds has a staff of about 400 people, according to Dr. Dobbins
He didn't state if this number was the total research and
development staff or if this was just the number employed in the
research division headed by Dr. Senkus
Dr. Schepartz, U.S.D.A., Eastern Regional Laboratories
Dr. Schepartz was quite interested in mycotoxins He referred
to the work of Dr. Forgacs published in SCIENCE this past summer
which dealt with mycotoxin from Timothy hay. Dr. Schepartz seemed
to be trying to interest a tobacco company in supporting additional
work by Dr. Forgacs Dr. Forgacs is retained as a consultant by
