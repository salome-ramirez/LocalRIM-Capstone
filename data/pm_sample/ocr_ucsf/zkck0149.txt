Recent emphasis on high resolution gradient echo studies in functional imaging has led to (he conclusion that 
there are likely three domains of response to the blood circulation in the brain when considering field 
in homogeneity effects of the venous blood pre- and during activation. The first is a coherent effect due to large or 
macroscopic vessels on Ihc order of the size of the voxel (ca 200-500 p In most studies). These enn lead to very 
large signal changes (up to 100%). The second is at the venule level (ca 50-200 nm) and is associated with smaller 
parenchymal changes (usually ca 10% or less). The third Is at the capillary level and is associated with much 
smaller signal changes at 1.5 T and even up to 4T. The actual signal changes depend on field strength and 
sequence design. In this paper, we present our experience in detecting the first two domains with 2D and 3D 
gradient echo Imaging at 1.5 T. We find that high resolution enables visualization of the larger small veins in 
motor cortex studies and that, on occasion, it is possible to sec the venule effects as well. We suggest a simple 
model to explain the large signal changes based on susceptibility changes and partial volume effects. 
Comparisons of the functional imaging data to this model and to MR angiographic studies are also shown as 
evidence of (he venous sources or the susceptibility changes.
