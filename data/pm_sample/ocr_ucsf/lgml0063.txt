A recent lY/ald Streetfournal/NBC
I-IArews survey found that the
loss of personal privacy is the issue
that concerns Americans most
.
about the new millennium . Loss
of privacy (29 percent) causes more
concern than terrorism and over-
population (both 23 percent),
world war (16 percent), and glob-
al warming (14 percent) .
Future worries aside, a review
of recent media coverage shows
the privacy issue today to be com-
plex, heavily publicized, a hotbed
for activists, and a growing topic
of consumer awareness . Gather-
ing and selling personal informa-
tion is not new, so why so many
red flags now? Easier, cheaper
technologies have opened the
floodgates to a huge amount of
personal information that is now
readily available to both govern-
ment and businesses .
How is the public reacting to
business and government peering
more and more into their lives?
According to recent consumer sur-
veys, in the marketing realm con-
sumers generally seem to be treat-
ing privacy as an"asset" that they
can choose to trade, barter, or sell .
In other areas-insurance, public
health, and employment new
conflicts are emerging between per-
sonal privacy interests and infor-
mation organizations . All of this is
leading to a growing body of fed-
eral and state law that tries to strike
a better balance between the two .
For corporate affairs profes-
sionals charged with helping our
businesses achieve their objec-
tives, there are both potential pit-
falls and opportunities present in
todays privacy arena . Examples of
both, as well as questions for us
to consider, are set forth in the fol-
lowing pages .
