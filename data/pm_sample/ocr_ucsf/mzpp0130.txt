Relevance of the N-nitroso Compounds from the Reaction of Primary
Amines with Nitrite and Thiocyanate to the Smoker
Tannenbaum et al . (1977Yconsider the reaction products of primary
amines in food on one hand and nitrite and thiocyanate, both pre-
sent in the saliva, on the other, to be "of potential significance
in the context of human cancer" . The authors present signifieant
evidence that N-nitroso compounds - N-nitroso dialkylamines and
N-nitroso cyano amines - are among those reaction products .
Primary amines are abundant in tobacco smoke (Neurath et al . 1966,
Pailler et al . 1967, Schmel~tz and Roffmann,'1977)' .
Nitrite is always present in human saliva, the quantity directly
correlated to the dietary intake of nitrate (Stephany and Schuller,
1975, Spiegelhalder et al . 1976, Tannenbaum et al . 1976) .
Thiocyanate has been found to be three to four times enhanced in
the aal~iva of smokers compared to its concentration in the saliva
of non-smokers (Densen et al . 1967, Boyl!and and Walker, 1974,
Walters et all . 1977) and is knovm to catalyse the nitroeation re-
action of secondary amines with nitrite, increasing the rate of
reaction about 550 times at px 1 .5 (Boyland'and :Walker, 1974) .
Therefore, the formation of N-nitroso compounds'from primary
amines in the smokers gastro-intestinal tract is to be considered
a potential second path of possible relevance of this class of
carcinogens to the smoker .
